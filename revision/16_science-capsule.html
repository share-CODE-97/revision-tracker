1 · Atomic Structure — Who Discovered What

Tap each scientist to reveal their discovery.

J. J. Thomson Chemistry
Discovered the Electron — using cathode ray tubes. Also proposed the "plum pudding" model of the atom.
E. Goldstein Chemistry
Discovered the Proton — observed canal rays (positive rays) in a discharge tube.
James Chadwick Chemistry
Discovered the Neutron — a neutral particle in the nucleus. Awarded Nobel Prize in 1935.
Ernest Rutherford Physics
Discovered the Nucleus — through the gold foil experiment (α-particle scattering). Also proposed the nuclear model of the atom and proved that most of the atom is empty space.
Mnemonic — "T-G-C-R"
Thomson → Electron · Goldstein → Proton · Chadwick → Neutron · Rutherford → Nucleus

3 · Radioactivity — Alpha, Beta & Gamma Rays

Tap to reveal how each ray behaves in electric and magnetic fields.

Alpha (α) Rays Radioactive
Positively charged (+). Deflected by electric and magnetic fields. Heavy — least penetrating, most ionising.
Beta (β) Rays Radioactive
Negatively charged (−). Deflected by electric and magnetic fields (in the opposite direction to alpha). Medium penetration.
Gamma (γ) Rays Radioactive
Neutral — no charge. Not deflected by electric or magnetic fields. Pure electromagnetic radiation, highest penetration, lowest ionising power.
Trick to remember deflection: Only charged particles deflect. Alpha (+) and Beta (−) deflect — Gamma (0) doesn't. "Charged → deflects; neutral → straight through."

4 · Radioisotopes & Their Uses

Iodine-131 Medical
Used in the treatment of thyroid diseases (including thyroid cancer). The thyroid gland absorbs iodine.
Cobalt-60 Medical
Used in the treatment of cancer (radiotherapy). Also used to sterilise medical equipment.
Uranium-235 Nuclear
Used as nuclear reactor fuel and in nuclear weapons. Undergoes fission with slow neutrons.
Exam tip:

If the question asks "which radioisotope is used for thyroid?" → Iodine-131.

If it asks "for cancer treatment?" → Cobalt-60.

If it asks "for nuclear reactors?" → Uranium-235.

5 · Periodic Table — f-block Elements

f-block Elements

Lanthanides (Z = 57 – 71) — also called rare earth metals. Belong to the f-block.

Actinides (Z = 89 – 103) — all radioactive. Also called inner transition elements.

Note: Elements with atomic number 71 to 103 are mostly f-block (actinides).

Quick fact: Lanthanides + Actinides together = f-block = inner transition elements. Positioned separately at the bottom of the periodic table.

6 · Metals & Non-metals — Special Cases

Liquid Metal Metal
Mercury (Hg) — the only metal that is liquid at room temperature. Also called "quick silver". (Gallium melts at 29.5°C — it becomes liquid above room temperature, so it's not the standard answer.)
Liquid Non-metal Non-metal
Bromine (Br) — the only non-metal that is liquid at room temperature. Reddish-brown, volatile.
Best Conductors of Electricity (in order) Physics
Ag > Cu > Au > Al

Silver (Ag) is the best conductor, but Copper (Cu) is used in wires because it's cheaper and more abundant.

Mnemonic: "Silver, Copper, Gold, Aluminium" — remember them in order Ag-Cu-Au-Al.

8 · Important Chemicals

Sulphuric Acid (H₂SO₄) Chemical
Called the "King of Chemicals" because it is the most widely used industrial chemical — in fertilisers, batteries, detergents, dyes, petroleum refining, etc.
Galvanization Process
Galvanization prevents the rusting of iron by coating it with a protective layer of zinc (Zn). Zinc acts as a sacrificial metal — it corrodes first, protecting the iron underneath.

9 · Ores & Their Metals

Tap each ore to reveal its metal. Note: some entries in your notes were corrected below.

Bauxite
Aluminium (Al) — the main ore of aluminium. Formula: Al₂O₃·2H₂O.
Cassiterite
Tin (Sn) — not copper. Formula: SnO₂.
Cinnabar
Mercury (Hg) — not zinc. Formula: HgS. Bright red mineral.
Galena
Lead (Pb) — not gold. Formula: PbS. The main ore of lead.
⚠ Common mix-ups to remember:

• Cassiterite → Tin (not Copper — that's Copper pyrite)

• Cinnabar → Mercury (not Zinc — that's Zinc blende)

• Galena → Lead (not Gold — that's Calaverite)

• Convex lens → forms both real and virtual images (real when object is beyond F).
