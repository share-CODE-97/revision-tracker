Mountains & Volcanoes

Ranges · highest peaks of continents · active / dormant / extinct volcanoes — tap to reveal.

Two separate topics, one page.
  • Mountains — physical features (ranges, peaks, continents).
  • Volcanoes — classified by activity: Active (erupts often), Dormant (sleeping but can wake), Extinct (dead, won't erupt again).
UPSC / BPSC loves asking: highest peak of a continent, and whether a volcano is active / dormant / extinct.

1 · Major Mountain Ranges of the World

Read the range name → say the continent and the highest peak → tap to check.

Rockies North America

Location: Western USA and Canada — stretch from Alaska down to New Mexico.

Highest peak: Mount Elbert — 4401 m (Colorado, USA).

Famous for: Yellowstone, Grand Teton and Rocky Mountain National Parks.

Appalachians North America

Location: Eastern USA and Canada — the oldest mountain system of North America.

Highest peak: Mount Mitchell — 2037 m (North Carolina, USA).

Famous for: The Appalachian Trail — the world's longest hiking trail.

Scandinavian Mountains Europe

Location: Norway and Sweden (the Scandinavian peninsula).

Highest peak: Galdhøpiggen — 2469 m (Norway) — the highest peak of Northern Europe.

Famous for: Fjords, glaciers, and the northern lights.

Alps Europe

Location: Stretch across France, Switzerland, Italy, Austria, Germany, Slovenia.

Highest peak: Mont Blanc — 4809 m (France / Italy border).

Famous for: Europe's most popular ski region; the Matterhorn; the Rhine, Rhône, and Po rivers all rise here.

Urals EuropeAsia

Location: Russia — the classic boundary between Europe and Asia.

Highest peak: Mount Narodnaya — 1894 m.

Famous for: Rich mineral belt (iron, copper, gold, platinum). The Urals mark the continental divide of Eurasia.

Atlas Africa

Location: Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia — the "backbone" of North Africa.

Highest peak: Mount Toubkal — 4167 m (Morocco).

Famous for: Separating the Mediterranean coast from the Sahara desert.

Andes South America

Location: Runs the entire length of South America — 7000 km — through Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Chile, Argentina.

Highest peak: Mount Aconcagua — 6961 m (Argentina) — the highest peak in the Western and Southern Hemispheres.

Famous for: The world's longest mountain range. Site of the Atacama desert and the Amazon's source.

Drakensberg Africa

Location: South Africa (also Lesotho and Eswatini).

Highest peak: Thabana Ntlenyana — 3482 m (Lesotho).

Famous for: "Drakensberg" means "Dragon's Mountain" in Afrikaans.

Great Dividing Range Australia

Location: Eastern Australia — Queensland, NSW, Victoria.

Highest peak: Mount Kosciuszko — 2228 m (New South Wales) — the highest peak of mainland Australia.

Famous for: The only major mountain range in Australia.

Altai Asia

Location: Russia, Mongolia, China, Kazakhstan.

Highest peak: Belukha Mountain — 4506 m.

Famous for: The source region of the Ob and Irtysh rivers; rich in snow leopards.

Himalayas Asia

Location: India, Nepal, Bhutan, Tibet (China), Pakistan.

Highest peak: Mount Everest — 8849 m (Nepal – Tibet border) — the highest peak in the world.

Famous for: The world's youngest and highest fold mountains. Home of the "Third Pole" (glaciers). Source of Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra.

3 · Volcanoes — three families

Active

Erupts frequently or has erupted in recent history. Still alive and dangerous.

Dormant

"Sleeping" — no eruption for a long time, but can erupt again. Not dead.

Extinct

Dead — no recorded eruption in historic times, and no magma supply left.

3A · Active Volcanoes

Tap each to reveal its country.

Mt. Taal Active
Philippines. Located on Luzon island, inside a lake — one of the smallest active volcanoes in the world.
Mt. Mayon Active
Philippines. Famous for its almost-perfect cone — "the world's most perfect volcano".
Mt. Pinatubo Active
Philippines. Its 1991 eruption was one of the largest of the 20th century — cooled the entire planet by ~0.5 °C for a year.
Barren Island Active
Andaman & Nicobar Islands, India — India's only active volcano. Lies in the Bay of Bengal.
Stromboli Active
Italy. Called the "Lighthouse of the Mediterranean" — erupts almost continuously.
Mt. Etna Active
Italy (Sicily). Europe's most active volcano — erupts almost every year.
Hekla Active
Iceland. In medieval Europe it was called the "Gateway to Hell".
Laki Active
Iceland. Its 1783 eruption killed ~25% of Iceland's population and caused crop failures across Europe.
Katmai Active
Alaska, USA. Site of the 1912 Novarupta eruption — the largest of the 20th century.
Mt. Rainier Active
Washington, USA. Considered one of the most dangerous volcanoes in the USA.
Mt. Shasta Active
California, USA. Part of the Cascade Range.
Mauna Loa Active
Hawaii, USA. The largest volcano on Earth by volume.
Mauna Kea Active
Hawaii, USA. Taller than Everest when measured from its base on the ocean floor.
Kilauea Active
Hawaii, USA. One of the world's most active volcanoes — erupts almost non-stop.
Mt. Pelée Active
West Indies (Martinique). Its 1902 eruption destroyed the city of Saint-Pierre — only 3 survivors.
Cotopaxi Active
Ecuador. One of the world's highest active volcanoes (5897 m).
Erebus Active
Antarctica. The southernmost active volcano on Earth — has a permanent lava lake.

3B · Dormant Volcanoes

Narcondam Island Dormant
Andaman & Nicobar Islands, India — India's dormant volcano. Sits ~150 km from Barren Island.
Krakatoa Dormant
Indonesia. Its 1883 eruption was one of the deadliest in history — heard 3000+ km away.
Mt. Fujiyama / Fuji Dormant
Japan. Japan's highest peak (3776 m) and a sacred symbol.
Vesuvius Dormant
Italy. Famous for the 79 AD eruption that buried Pompeii and Herculaneum.

3C · Extinct Volcanoes

Mt. Popa Extinct
Myanmar (Burma). A sacred pilgrimage site — "Mount Popa" is an extinct volcano.
Mt. Damavand Extinct
Iran. The highest peak in Iran and the Middle East (5610 m).
Koh Sultan Extinct
Iran. A dormant / extinct volcanic cone in the eastern Iranian plateau.
Kilimanjaro Extinct
Tanzania (border with Kenya). The highest peak in Africa (5895 m) — a dormant volcano that may erupt again.
Aconcagua Extinct
Argentina – Chile border. Highest peak in South America (6962 m) — an extinct volcano.
🇮🇳 India-only revision:

Barren Island — India's only active volcano. In the Andaman Sea, ~138 km from Port Blair.

Narcondam Island — India's dormant volcano. Also in the Andaman Sea.

Both are part of the Andaman & Nicobar Islands union territory. UPSC has asked this repeatedly.

4 · Memory tricks that save you

Trick 1 — The 7 highest peaks, in descending order:
"Everest · Aconcagua · McKinley · Kilimanjaro · Elbrus · Vinson · Kosciuszko"
Remember the mnemonic: "E-A-M-K-E-V-K" → "Every Adventurous Mountaineer Knows Every Very High Kilometre".
Trick 2 — Continents + their peaks:
Asia → Everest (highest)
South America → Aconcagua
North America → McKinley
Africa → Kilimanjaro
Europe → Elbrus
Antarctica → Vinson
Australia → Kosciuszko (lowest)
Trick 3 — Volcano types by country:
• Philippines has three: Taal, Mayon, Pinatubo — "T-M-P".
• Italy has three: Stromboli, Etna (active), Vesuvius (dormant).
• Iceland has two: Hekla, Laki — "H-L".
• Hawaii has three: Mauna Loa, Mauna Kea, Kilauea — "M-M-K".
• India has two: Barren Island (active), Narcondam (dormant).
Trick 4 — "Extinct" club:
"Popa (Myanmar) · Damavand (Iran) · Koh Sultan (Iran) · Kilimanjaro (Tanzania) · Aconcagua (Argentina)"
Memory: Extinct = East + Africa + South America.
Trick 5 — The one that catches everyone:
Barren Island = active (India).
Narcondam = dormant (India).
Both in Andaman & Nicobar Islands. Do not swap them.
Trick 6 — Ranges vs highest peak:
• Andes — world's longest range → Aconcagua.
• Himalayas — world's highest range → Everest.
• Rockies — North America → Elbert.
• Appalachians — oldest in North America → Mitchell.
• Alps — Europe → Mont Blanc.
• Urals — Europe-Asia divide → Narodnaya.

While the extraction of the information from the provided map is complete, **the map itself is not comprehensive enough for BPSC or UPSC preparation**. The image provides a basic foundational overview, but Civil Services examinations require a much deeper understanding of geomorphology, drainage, and ecology. To elevate this topic to a BPSC/UPSC standard, you must supplement the map's data with the following critical details for each region: ## 1. The Himalayas & Trans-Himalayas * **Formation:** They are Young Fold Mountains formed by the tectonic collision of the Indian Plate with the Eurasian Plate. * **Trans-Himalayas (Karakoram, Ladakh, Zaskar):** Contains major glaciers like Siachen, Baltoro, and Biafo. The Ladakh range separates the Indus and Shyok river systems. * **Regional Divisions:** You must know the longitudinal divisions (Punjab Himalayas, Kumaon Himalayas, Nepal Himalayas, and Assam Himalayas) and the rivers that demarcate them (Indus, Sutlej, Kali, Tista, Dihang). * **Syntaxial Bends:** The sharp southward bends of the Himalayas at their western (Nanga Parbat) and eastern (Namcha Barwa) extremities. * **Important Passes:** Questions frequently ask to match passes with states or ranges (e.g., Zoji La, Rohtang, Nathu La, Bomdi La, Lipulekh). ## 2. Central Indian Highlands * **Aravalli Range:** These are the oldest fold mountains in India, now reduced to relict (residual) mountains due to extensive weathering. They act as a water divide between the Indus basin and the Ganga basin, and are the source of rivers like Luni and Banas. * **Vindhya Range:** Acts as the traditional boundary between North and South India. It is a block mountain system and is a major source of rivers like Chambal, Betwa, and Ken. Its highest point is Sadbhawna Shikhar (Goodwill Peak). * **Satpura Range:** A series of seven mountains (Sat-pura). It is a block mountain flanked by rift valleys (Narmada to the north, Tapi to the south). Its highest peak is **Dhupgarh** (1,350 m) in the Mahadeo Hills. ## 3. The Peninsular Plateau & Coastal Mountains * **Western Ghats (Sahyadris):** * **Geology:** They are essentially the faulted edge of the Deccan Plateau (escarpments), not true mountains. * **Continuity:** They are continuous and can only be crossed through specific passes (Gaps): **Thal Ghat** (connects Mumbai-Nashik), **Bhor Ghat** (Mumbai-Pune), and **Palghat** (Kerala-Tamil Nadu). * **Ecology:** A UNESCO World Heritage site and a globally recognized biodiversity hotspot. * **Drainage:** The primary watershed of the peninsula, sourcing major east-flowing rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri). * **Eastern Ghats:** * **Geology:** Older than the Western Ghats and highly discontinuous. They have been heavily eroded and cut through by the major east-flowing rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Pennar, Kaveri) emptying into the Bay of Bengal. * **Highest Peak:** **Jindhagada Peak** (1,690 m) in Andhra Pradesh (though Mahendragiri is also frequently cited in older NCERTs). * **Nilgiri Hills (Blue Mountains):** The structural knot where the Eastern Ghats and Western Ghats meet. Famous for the Shola forests and the Kurinji flower.
