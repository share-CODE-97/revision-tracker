Cropping Seasons in India

Kharif · Rabi · Zaid — India's three agricultural seasons, crop-wise, region-wise, exam-ready.

1. The Big Picture

India's cropping calendar is driven by the monsoon. The Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) classifies the agricultural year into three main seasons — plus a fourth "year-long" category for crops like sugarcane.

KHARIF June – September (Monsoon)
RABI October – March (Winter)
ZAID April – June (Summer)

3. Season-wise Deep Dive

3.1 Kharif — the Monsoon Crops

  • Sown with the onset of the South-West monsoon (June–July).
  • Harvested in September–October.
  • Needs high temperature + high humidity at sowing, and warm dry weather at harvest.

Major crops:

Paddy · Maize · Jowar · Bajra · Ragi · Tur (Arhar) · Moong · Urad · Cotton · Jute · Groundnut · Soyabean · Sunflower · Sesamum · Nigerseed

Exam angle: Kharif crops are also called monsoon crops. Remember: cotton and jute are the two most-quoted commercial Kharif crops.

3.2 Rabi — the Winter Crops

  • Sown in winter (October–December), after the monsoon retreats.
  • Harvested in March–April (the "harvest" festival season).
  • Requires cool growing season + bright sunshine at ripening.

Major crops:

Wheat · Barley · Peas · Gram (Chana) · Mustard / Rapeseed · Masur (Lentil) · Safflower

Exam angle: Wheat is the most important Rabi crop of India. Rabi crops are also called winter crops.

3.3 Zaid — the Summer Crops

  • Short season between Rabi and Kharif (April–June).
  • Grown on irrigated land, requires warm dry weather with ample water supply.

Major crops:

Watermelon · Muskmelon · Cucumber · Bitter gourd · Pumpkin · Ridged gourd · Vegetables · Fodder crops

Year-long crop: Sugarcane takes almost a full year to grow — it does not fit neatly into Kharif / Rabi / Zaid.

5. North vs South — Key Contrast

North Indian States

  • Sharp season division (cold winters, hot summers).
  • Dominant Kharif: Rice, Cotton, Bajra, Maize, Jowar, Arhar.
  • Dominant Rabi: Wheat, Gram, Torai, Mustard, Barley.
  • Zaid: mainly Vegetables, Fruits, Fodder.

South Indian States

  • Tropical climate → less seasonal contrast.
  • Rice, Maize, Ragi, Jowar, Groundnut grown in multiple seasons.
  • Ragi (finger millet) is a South-India speciality.
  • Zaid includes Rice + Vegetables + Fodder.

BPSC / UPSC tip: Questions usually test — (i) which crop belongs to which season, (ii) sowing/harvest months, (iii) North-vs-South crop split, and (iv) odd-one-out (e.g. "Sugarcane is grown in which season?" → none of the three; it's a year-long crop).
