1. Major Mineral Belts of India

India's mineral wealth is highly uneven, concentrated primarily in the peninsular plateau region, ancient crystalline rocks, and sedimentary basins.

North-Eastern Peninsular Belt

Regions: Chota Nagpur Plateau (Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha)[cite: 1].

Significance: Known as the "Ruhr of India". This is the richest mineral belt.

Key Minerals: Massive concentrations of Coal (⚫), Iron (◯), Manganese (➕), and Mica (🟪)[cite: 1].

Central Belt

Regions: Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh[cite: 1].

Significance: The second most significant belt, serving central and southern heavy industries.

Key Minerals: Manganese (➕), Bauxite (▲), Limestone (★), and Coal (⚫)[cite: 1].

Southern Belt

Regions: Karnataka Plateau, contiguous parts of Tamil Nadu and Kerala[cite: 1].

Significance: Highly rich in ferrous minerals but distinctively lacks significant coal deposits.

Key Minerals: Gold (🟢), Iron (◯), Bauxite (▲), and Limestone (★)[cite: 1].

North-Western Belt

Regions: Aravalli region in Rajasthan and adjacent areas of Gujarat[cite: 1].

Significance: Dominated by non-ferrous metals and non-metallic building materials.

Key Minerals: Lead & Zinc (✳), Copper (◽), Gypsum (▲), and Petroleum (🟡) in Gujarat[cite: 1].

2. Detailed Distribution Analysis

Ferrous & Non-Ferrous Metals Metallic

Mineral Primary States / Regions UPSC Strategic Note
Iron Ore (◯) Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Goa[cite: 1] Backbone of modern industrialization. India has vast high-grade hematite & magnetite reserves.
Manganese (➕) Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh[cite: 1] Crucial for steel smelting and manufacturing ferro-alloys.
Bauxite (▲) Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Meghalaya[cite: 1] Ore of Aluminum. Usually found in laterite soil regions (plateau edges and hills).
Copper (◽) Rajasthan (Khetri), Madhya Pradesh (Malanjkhand), Jharkhand (Singhbhum), Sikkim[cite: 1] India is structurally deficient in Copper, requiring heavy imports despite these reserves.
Lead & Zinc (✳) Rajasthan (almost monopolistic concentration), small pockets in Sikkim & Meghalaya[cite: 1] Found together in nature. Rajasthan is the undisputed leader[cite: 1].
Gold (🟢) Karnataka, border of Kerala/TN, Andhra Pradesh[cite: 1] Highly localized (e.g., Kolar, Hutti). India imports massive amounts to meet domestic demand.

Non-Metallic & Industrial Minerals Non-Metallic

  • Mica (🟪): Concentrated in Jharkhand, Rajasthan, and Andhra Pradesh[cite: 1]. Vital for the electrical/electronics industry due to its insulating properties.
  • Limestone (★): Widely distributed across almost all states including Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, AP, Karnataka, Gujarat, Meghalaya, and Assam[cite: 1]. The foundational raw material for the cement industry.
  • Dolomite (🟥): Mined in MP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, and Rajasthan[cite: 1]. Used as a flux in the iron and steel industry.
  • Gypsum (▲): Prominently found in arid/semi-arid regions of Rajasthan, Gujarat, and parts of J&K and Tamil Nadu[cite: 1]. Essential for fertilizers and cement.
  • Asbestos (🟦): Highly restricted, mostly found in Rajasthan and Andhra Pradesh[cite: 1].

Energy Minerals Energy

  • Coal (⚫): Predominantly in the eastern belt (Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, Chhattisgarh), extending into MP and Telangana[cite: 1]. Tertiary coal is found in the North-East (Assam, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh)[cite: 1].
  • Petroleum & Natural Gas (🟡): Located in sedimentary basins and offshore regions. Key areas include Gujarat (Cambay), Assam/Arunachal Pradesh (Digboi/Naharkatiya), and Tamil Nadu (Cauvery basin)[cite: 1].

3. Prelims Quick Revision Traps

  • Trap 1: Do not assume the North-East is barren. It holds strategic reserves of Coal (⚫), Petroleum (🟡), Limestone (★), and even Bauxite (▲)[cite: 1].
  • Trap 2: Rajasthan is not just desert; it is exceptionally rich in non-ferrous and non-metallic minerals like Lead/Zinc, Copper, Gypsum, and Mica[cite: 1].
  • Trap 3: South India lacks extensive Coal (⚫) reserves (except lignite in Neyveli, TN), but makes up for it with rich Iron (◯) and Gold (🟢) deposits[cite: 1].
