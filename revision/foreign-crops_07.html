Crops Introduced by Foreigners

Portuguese, British, Arabs & Mughals — who brought what, and where it came from. High-yield for UPSC Prelims & BPSC.

Quick Origin Map

Who Introduced What

  • Portuguese — Potato, Tomato, Chili, Tobacco, Maize, Pineapple, Papaya, Cashew, Guava, Custard Apple, Tapioca, Groundnut, Cocoa (maximum New World crops)
  • British — Rubber, Tea, Cinchona, Apple (temperate varieties)
  • Arabs — Coffee (via Baba Budan to Karnataka)
  • Persians & Mughals — Grapes (improved varieties)
  • Europeans — Sunflower

One-Liners You Must Know

  • Portuguese introduced the maximum number of New World crops into India.
  • Chili replaced traditional Indian spices like long pepper in many regions.
  • Baba Budan is associated with the introduction of coffee in India.
  • British commercialized tea and rubber plantations.
  • Cashew was initially introduced to prevent soil erosion on coastal slopes.

Regions to Remember

🍵 Tea: Assam, Darjeeling, Nilgiris ☕ Coffee: Karnataka, Kerala 🌳 Rubber: Kerala 🥜 Cashew: Goa, Kerala, Karnataka 🥔 Tapioca: Kerala 🍎 Apple: Himachal Pradesh, J&K

BPSC / UPSC High-Yield Focus

Theme What to Remember
Columbian Exchange The exchange of plants, animals, culture, and technology between the Old World (Asia, Africa, Europe) and the New World (Americas) after the Voyages of Discovery. Portuguese were the primary conduits for this into India.
Portuguese vs British Portuguese introduced food crops (potato, tomato, chili, maize). British introduced commercial/plantation crops (tea, rubber, cinchona).
Baba Budan A Sufi saint who smuggled seven coffee beans from Yemen to Karnataka (Chikmagalur) in the 17th century. The birthplace of Indian coffee.
Cinchona & Quinine Cinchona (source of quinine) was introduced by the British to combat malaria. Cultivated in the Nilgiris and Darjeeling hills.
Kharif vs Rabi Maize is a major kharif crop. Potato and wheat are rabi crops. This is a frequent classification question.
Plantation Agriculture Tea, coffee, rubber, and cinchona are plantation crops — characterized by large estates, capital investment, and processing on-site.
Memory hook for Portuguese crops: "P-T-C-T-M-P-P-C-G-C-T-G-C" — Potato, Tomato, Chili, Tobacco, Maize, Pineapple, Papaya, Cashew, Guava, Custard Apple, Tapioca, Groundnut, Cocoa.
British crops: "R-T-C-A" — Rubber, Tea, Cinchona, Apple.
Remember: Portuguese came first (16th century), British came later (18th–19th century). Portuguese gave us food; British gave us cash crops.
