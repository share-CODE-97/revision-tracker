Viceroys of British India & Their Work

1858–1947. Eight Crown representatives who shaped the Raj, from the aftermath of the 1857 Revolt to the Radcliffe Line.

1 · The Crown Takes Over — Lord Canning (1858–1862)

The first Viceroy. The man who inherited the rubble of 1857 and laid the foundation of the Raj.

Lord Canning First Viceroy 1858–1862
① First Viceroy of India — the Crown took over from the East India Company under the Government of India Act 1858. Canning had been Governor-General since 1856, but became the first Viceroy in 1858. ② Aftermath of 1857: Oversaw the suppression of the revolt, the exile of Bahadur Shah Zafar, and the policy of clemency towards the rebels who surrendered. ③ Indian Councils Act, 1861: Introduced the portfolio system (each member of the Viceroy's Council got a department) and began the association of Indians with law-making. ④ Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1858): Read out at Allahabad, promising non-interference in religion and equal treatment of Indians in government service. ⑤ Universities of Bombay, Madras, and Calcutta (1857): Established based on Charles Wood's Despatch of 1854.

2 · The Age of Reform — Lord Ripon (1880–1884)

The most liberal Viceroy of the 19th century — and the one who made the British nervous.

Lord Ripon Reformer 1880–1884
① Local Self-Government (1882): Called the "Father of Local Self-Government in India". His resolution of 1882 laid the foundation of elected municipal and district boards. ② Ilbert Bill (1883): Sought to allow Indian judges to try European offenders. The white backlash against the bill forced a compromise — the "concordat" of 1883. This is the single most-asked fact about Ripon. ③ Repealed the Vernacular Press Act (1882): The Act had been passed by Lord Lytton in 1878 to gag Indian-language newspapers. Ripon repealed it — a landmark for press freedom. ④ First Factory Act (1881): Regulated child labour — prohibited employment of children under 7 and limited working hours for those aged 7–12. ⑤ Hunter Commission (1882): Appointed to review the state of education in India, especially primary and secondary education.
V.Imp — Ripon's legacy: Ripon is remembered as the most pro-Indian Viceroy. His Ilbert Bill attempted to remove racial discrimination in the judiciary. The backlash from the European community in India was so fierce that the bill had to be watered down. This is the classic question of "who tried to give Indian judges power over Europeans?" — Lord Ripon.

3 · The Communal Divide — Lord Minto II (1905–1910)

The Viceroy who institutionalised separate electorates and set India on the road to Partition.

Lord Minto II Communal 1905–1910
① Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909): Introduced separate electorates for Muslims — the single most consequential reform of the pre-Gandhian era. This institutionalised the communal divide. ② Muslim League formation (1906): Founded at Dacca (Dhaka) in December 1906. The British encouraged it as a counterweight to the Congress. ③ Surat Split (1907): The Congress split into Moderates and Extremists at Surat. Minto's tenure saw the Congress at its weakest. ④ Newspapers Act (1908): Used to suppress nationalist newspapers. Tilak was tried for sedition and sentenced to six years' transportation (Mandalay). ⑤ Indian Councils Act 1909: Also enlarged the legislative councils and allowed Indians to be associated with the executive councils. But the separate electorate clause is what Prelims remembers.
V.Imp — Separate Electorates: The Morley-Minto Reforms (1909) introduced separate electorates for Muslims. This was later extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, and Europeans — and finally to the depressed classes under the Communal Award (1932). The seed of Partition is planted in 1909.

4 · War & Mass Movements — Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921)

The Viceroy of the Jallianwala Bagh massacre, the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms, and the first all-India mass movement.

Lord Chelmsford War & Mass 1916–1921
① Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919): Introduced dyarchy in the provinces — subjects divided into transferred (run by Indian ministers) and reserved (run by the Governor). ② Rowlatt Act (1919): Officially the Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act. Allowed detention without trial. Gandhi called it the "Black Act". ③ Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919): General Dyer ordered firing on a peaceful gathering in Amritsar. Officially 379 killed. The Hunter Committee (1919) investigated. ④ Khilafat & Non-Cooperation Movement (1919–22): Gandhi supported the Khilafat Movement, and the Non-Cooperation Movement was launched in 1920. It was withdrawn in 1922 after Chauri Chaura. ⑤ Government of India Act, 1919: Also introduced direct elections, separated central and provincial budgets, and provided for a Central Public Service Commission (established 1926).

5 · The Communal Award & Poona Pact — Lord Willingdon (1931–1936)

The Viceroy who crushed the Civil Disobedience Movement and presided over the Gandhi–Ambedkar negotiation.

Lord Willingdon Repression 1931–1936
① Second Round Table Conference (1931): Held in London. Gandhi was the sole representative of the Congress. Deadlock over the communal question — Gandhi rejected separate electorates for the depressed classes; Ambedkar insisted on them. ② Crushed the Civil Disobedience Movement: After the failure of the Second RTC, the movement was relaunched (1932) and then withdrawn in 1934. Willingdon's government arrested Gandhi and thousands of others. ③ Communal Award (16 August 1932): Announced by Ramsay MacDonald — extended separate electorates to the depressed classes (Dalits). ④ Poona Pact (24 September 1932): Between Gandhi and Ambedkar. Separate electorates were withdrawn; reserved seats in joint electorates were increased (from 71 to 147 in provincial legislatures). ⑤ Government of India Act, 1935: Passed during his tenure — introduced provincial autonomy (replacing dyarchy in provinces), dyarchy at the centre, an All-India Federation (never implemented), and a Federal Court.
V.Imp — The Poona Pact: The Communal Award (1932) gave separate electorates to the depressed classes. Gandhi began a fast unto death. The Poona Pact (24 Sep 1932) between Gandhi and Ambedkar withdrew separate electorates for Dalits and increased reserved seats in joint electorates from 71 to 147. This is a permanent Prelims question.

6 · The Endgame Begins — Lord Linlithgow (1936–1943)

The longest-serving Viceroy. The man who took India into World War II without consulting Indians — and faced Quit India.

Lord Linlithgow War & Quit India 1936–1943
① August Offer (8 August 1940): Promised dominion status as the objective, expansion of the Executive Council with more Indians, a constituent assembly after the war, and minority safeguards. Rejected by both Congress and the Muslim League. ② Individual Satyagraha (1940–41): Launched by Gandhi. Acharya Vinoba Bhave was the first satyagrahi; Jawaharlal Nehru was the second. ③ Cripps Mission (March 1942): Led by Sir Stafford Cripps. Offered dominion status after the war and an option for provinces to stay out of the Union. Rejected by both Congress and the League. ④ Quit India Movement (8 August 1942): Launched at Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay. Gandhi's slogan: "Do or Die". The entire Congress leadership was arrested within hours. ⑤ Famine of Bengal (1943): The Bengal famine occurred during his tenure — over 2 million deaths. A permanent stain on his record.

7 · The Final Act — Lord Wavell (1943–1947) & Lord Mountbatten (1947)

Two men, four years, and the end of the Raj.

Lord Wavell Endgame 1943–1947
① Wavell Plan (June 1945): Proposed a reconstituted Executive Council with all members Indians except the Viceroy and Commander-in-Chief. Failed over the Muslim League's demand to nominate all Muslim members. ② Simla Conference (1945): Called by Wavell to agree on the Executive Council. Failed because of the Congress–League deadlock. ③ Cabinet Mission (1946): Three British cabinet ministers (Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps, Alexander) proposed a union of India with a weak centre. Collapsed over the grouping issue. ④ Interim Government (2 September 1946): Formed with Jawaharlal Nehru as Vice-President of the Executive Council (effectively Prime Minister). ⑤ Constituent Assembly (9 December 1946): First met in New Delhi. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President on 11 December 1946.
Lord Mountbatten Last Viceroy 1947
① Last Viceroy of India — March to August 1947. Also the first Governor-General of independent India (1947–48). ② Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947): Partition of India into two dominions. Provincial assemblies to decide their future. A Boundary Commission under Sir Cyril Radcliffe. ③ Indian Independence Act (18 July 1947): Passed by the British Parliament. Created two independent dominions — India and Pakistan — from 15 August 1947. Abolished the office of the Secretary of State for India. ④ Partition of India: The Radcliffe Line was published on 17 August 1947 — two days after independence. Displaced 14–15 million people; deaths estimated between 200,000 and 2 million. ⑤ "Jai Hind": Popularised by Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA. Coined by Major Abid Hasan Safrani.

8 · What was missing — the V.V.Imp additions for UPSC

Two Viceroys who are not in your notes but appear in almost every other Prelims paper.

Lord Curzon (1899–1905) V.Imp
① Partition of Bengal (1905): The most controversial act of his tenure. Split Bengal into Eastern Bengal and Assam (Muslim-majority) and West Bengal (Hindu-majority). Annulled in 1911.
② Indian Universities Act (1904): Tightened government control over universities — increased official nomination and put conditions on affiliation.
③ Ancient Monuments Preservation Act (1904): A landmark in heritage conservation. Appointed the first Archaeological Survey of India Director-General.
④ Delhi Durbar (1903): Held to commemorate the coronation of Edward VII. The capital was shifted from Calcutta to Delhi in 1911 — under Curzon's successor.
⑤ Curzon is called the "Father of the Indian Archaeological Survey" for his conservation efforts — but his Partition of Bengal sparked the Swadeshi Movement.
Lord Irwin (1926–1931) V.Imp
① Simon Commission (1927): Appointed under his tenure. All seven members were British — no Indian was included. Boycotted under the slogan "Simon Go Back".
② Nehru Report (1928): The first Indian-drafted constitution — chaired by Motilal Nehru. Recommended dominion status, not complete independence.
③ Lahore Session (December 1929): Purna Swaraj adopted; Jawaharlal Nehru presided. 26 January 1930 was declared the first Independence Day.
④ Dandi March (12 March 1930): Gandhi began the Salt Satyagraha from Sabarmati Ashram. Picked up salt at Dandi on 6 April 1930, launching the Civil Disobedience Movement.
⑤ Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931): Congress called off Civil Disobedience; British released political prisoners and allowed personal salt-making.

9 · Memory tricks that save you

The Viceroys in order — by role. "Canning Crowns, Ripon Reforms, Minto Divides, Chelmsford Represses, Willingdon Punishes, Linlithgow Defends, Wavell Warns, Mountbatten Wounds"
Canning = Crown takes over. Ripon = reforms (Ilbert Bill, Local Self-Government). Minto = divides (separate electorates). Chelmsford = represses (Jallianwala Bagh, Rowlatt Act). Willingdon = punishes (Communal Award, Poona Pact). Linlithgow = defends (Quit India, August Offer). Wavell = warns (Wavell Plan, Simla Conference). Mountbatten = wounds (Partition).
The Acts — by year and Viceroy. "1861 Councils, 1882 Local, 1909 Separate, 1919 Dyarchy, 1935 Autonomy"
1861 — Indian Councils Act (Canning). 1882 — Local Self-Government (Ripon). 1909 — Morley-Minto Reforms (Minto II) — separate electorates. 1919 — Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Chelmsford) — dyarchy in provinces. 1935 — Government of India Act (Willingdon) — provincial autonomy. Five acts, five dates, five Viceroys.
The three Round Table Conferences — who attended. "First no Congress, Second only Gandhi, Third no Gandhi"
1st RTC (Nov 1930, under Irwin): Congress boycotted — its leaders in jail. 2nd RTC (Sep 1931, under Willingdon): Gandhi the sole Congress delegate. 3rd RTC (Nov 1932, under Willingdon): Gandhi back in India, Congress not represented. The Congress did not attend the 3rd RTC.
The three British plans — in order. "Cripps, Wavell, Cabinet"
Cripps Mission (March 1942, under Linlithgow): Dominion status after the war; rejected by both Congress and League; triggered Quit India. Wavell Plan (June 1945): All-Indian Executive Council; failed over Muslim nomination. Cabinet Mission (1946): Three-tier federal union with groupings; collapsed over the grouping issue. Three plans, three failures, one road to Partition.
The last Viceroy and the last Governor-General. "Mountbatten the last Viceroy, Rajagopalachari the last Governor-General"
Lord Mountbatten = last Viceroy (March–August 1947) AND the first Governor-General of independent India (1947–48). C. Rajagopalachari = the last Governor-General (1948–1950) and the first Indian to hold the post. If the question asks "who was the last Governor-General", the answer is Rajaji — not Mountbatten.
The two most-confused Viceroys. "Minto divides, Chelmsford represses"
Minto II (1905–10) = Morley-Minto Reforms, separate electorates, Muslim League formation. Chelmsford (1916–21) = Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms, Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh, Khilafat, Non-Cooperation. Minto is the communal divider; Chelmsford is the wartime repressor.
Two corrections to the notes you shared. (1) Repealed Vernacular Press Act (1882) — this was Lord Ripon, not Lord Canning. The Act was passed by Lord Lytton in 1878 and repealed by Ripon in 1882. (2) Wavell Plan (1945) & Simla Conference (1945) — these were under Lord Wavell (1943–47), not Lord Linlithgow. Linlithgow's tenure ended in 1943. The Wavell Plan and Simla Conference belong to Wavell's viceroyalty.

10 · Rapid Fire

Cover the whole page. Answer out loud before you tap.

1. Who was the first Viceroy of India, and under which Act did the Crown take over?

Lord Canning (1858–1862). The Government of India Act, 1858 abolished the East India Company and transferred power to the British Crown.

2. Which Viceroy introduced the portfolio system, and in which year?

Lord Canning — Indian Councils Act, 1861. Each member of the Viceroy's Executive Council was given charge of a department.

3. Who is called the "Father of Local Self-Government in India"?

Lord Ripon — his resolution of 1882 laid the foundation of elected municipal and district boards.

4. What was the Ilbert Bill, and under which Viceroy was it passed?

Ilbert Bill (1883), under Lord Ripon. It sought to allow Indian judges to try European offenders. The white backlash forced a compromise — the "concordat" of 1883.

5. Who repealed the Vernacular Press Act, and in which year?

Lord Ripon, in 1882. The Act had been passed by Lord Lytton in 1878 to gag Indian-language newspapers.

6. Which Viceroy introduced separate electorates for Muslims, and under which Act?

Lord Minto II — Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909). This institutionalised the communal divide.

7. When was the Muslim League founded, and under which Viceroy?

30 December 1906, Dacca (Dhaka), under Lord Minto II. Moved by Nawab Salimullah Khan.

8. What happened at Jallianwala Bagh, when, and under which Viceroy?

13 April 1919, Amritsar, under Lord Chelmsford. General Reginald Dyer ordered firing on a peaceful gathering. Officially 379 killed. The Hunter Committee (1919) investigated.

9. Which Act introduced dyarchy in the provinces, and under which Viceroy?

Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act, 1919), under Lord Chelmsford. Subjects divided into transferred (run by Indian ministers) and reserved (run by the Governor).

10. What was the Rowlatt Act, and why was it called the "Black Act"?

Officially the Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919 — under Lord Chelmsford. It allowed detention without trial, trials without jury, and press restrictions. Gandhi called it the "Black Act".

11. Which Viceroy presided over the Second Round Table Conference, and who represented the Congress?

Lord Willingdon (1931). Gandhi was the sole representative of the Congress. Deadlock over the communal question — Gandhi rejected separate electorates for the depressed classes.

12. What was the Communal Award, who announced it, and what was the Poona Pact?

Communal Award — 16 August 1932, by Ramsay MacDonald; extended separate electorates to the depressed classes. The Poona Pact (24 Sep 1932) between Gandhi and Ambedkar withdrew separate electorates for Dalits and increased reserved seats in joint electorates from 71 to 147.

13. When was the Government of India Act 1935 passed, and what was its most important feature?

1935, under Lord Willingdon. Introduced provincial autonomy (replacing dyarchy in provinces), dyarchy at the centre, an All-India Federation (never implemented), and a Federal Court.

14. What was the August Offer, when was it made, and by whom?

8 August 1940, by Lord Linlithgow. Promised dominion status as the objective, expansion of the Executive Council, a constituent assembly after the war, and minority safeguards. Rejected by both Congress and the Muslim League.

15. Who was the first individual satyagrahi in 1940, and who was the second?

Acharya Vinoba Bhave was the first; Jawaharlal Nehru was the second. Over 25,000 satyagrahis were arrested. Under Lord Linlithgow.

16. When and where was the Quit India Movement launched, and what was Gandhi's slogan?

8 August 1942, Gowalia Tank Maidan (August Kranti Maidan), Bombay — under Lord Linlithgow. Gandhi's slogan was "Do or Die" (Karo ya Maro).

17. What was the Wavell Plan, when was it proposed, and what was the Simla Conference?

Wavell Plan — June 1945, under Lord Wavell. Proposed a reconstituted Executive Council with all members Indians except the Viceroy and Commander-in-Chief. The Simla Conference (1945) was called to discuss it. Failed over the Muslim League's demand to nominate all Muslim members.

18. What was the Cabinet Mission, who were its members, and what did it propose?

1946 — three British cabinet ministers: Pethick-Lawrence (Chairman), Stafford Cripps, and A. V. Alexander. Proposed a union of India with a weak centre, grouping of provinces, a constituent assembly, and an interim government. Collapsed over the grouping issue.

19. Who was the last Viceroy of India, and who was the last Governor-General?

Lord Mountbatten was the last Viceroy (March–August 1947) and the first Governor-General of independent India (1947–48). C. Rajagopalachari was the last Governor-General (1948–1950).

20. What did the Indian Independence Act 1947 do, and when did it receive royal assent?

Passed 18 July 1947 — created two independent dominions (India and Pakistan) from 15 August 1947, with the right to secede from the Commonwealth. Abolished the office of the Secretary of State for India and ended British suzerainty over the princely states.

21. Who drew the Radcliffe Line, when was it published, and what was its consequence?

Sir Cyril Radcliffe — a British lawyer who had never been to India. The line was published 17 August 1947, two days after independence. It displaced 14–15 million people; deaths are estimated between 200,000 and 2 million.

22. Who partitioned Bengal, in which year, and when was it annulled?

Lord Curzon, in 1905. Annulled in 1911 at the Delhi Durbar of George V — when the capital was also shifted from Calcutta to Delhi.

23. Who was the Viceroy during the Simon Commission boycott, and what was the slogan?

Lord Irwin (1926–1931). The Simon Commission (1927) was boycotted because all seven members were British — no Indian was included. Slogan: "Simon Go Back".

24. What was the Gandhi-Irwin Pact, when was it signed, and what did each side concede?

5 March 1931, between Gandhi and Lord Irwin. Congress agreed to call off Civil Disobedience and attend the 2nd RTC. British agreed to release political prisoners (except those convicted of violence), allow personal salt-making, and return confiscated property.
