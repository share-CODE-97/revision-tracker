Major Grasslands of the the World

Names · Continents · Countries — tap any card to reveal.

One-line definition: A grassland is a region where rainfall is too low for forests but too high for deserts — so the dominant plant life is grass. They are called by different names on different continents, and UPSC/Bihar PCS loves asking "Prairies are in which country?" or "Velds are the grasslands of which country?".
The one map you must fix in your head:
  • Americas → Prairies (North), Pampas / Llanos / Campos (South)
  • Europe → Puszta (Hungary), Steppes (Eurasia)
  • Asia → Steppes (Russia/Central Asia), Manchuria (China)
  • Africa → Savanna (whole continent), Velds (South Africa)
  • Australia → Downs
  • New Zealand → Canterbury

1 · The five big ones (most asked)

Read the name → say the continent + country → tap to check.

Prairies North America

Country: USA and Canada (the Great Plains region).

Climate: Temperate grassland. Hot summers, cold winters, moderate rainfall.

Famous for: Wheat basket of the world — the biggest wheat-growing belt on Earth. Also called the "Granary of the World".

Wildlife: Bison, prairie dogs, coyotes.

Steppes EuropeAsia

Region: The Eurasian Steppe — stretching from Ukraine/Russia, through Central Asia (Kazakhstan, Mongolia), into China.

Climate: Continental, semi-arid. Cold winters, hot summers.

Famous for: Horse-rearing (the historic "Steppe nomads" — Mongols, Scythians).

Wildlife: Antelopes, saiga, wild horses.

Pampas South America

Country: Argentina (also parts of Uruguay and Brazil).

Climate: Temperate grassland — mild winters, warm summers, good rainfall.

Famous for: The Gauchos (cowboys) — Argentina's cattle-ranching culture. Also called the "Wheat bowl of South America".

Wildlife: Pampas deer, armadillos, rhea.

Velds Africa

Country: South Africa.

Climate: Subtropical — warm and dry. Divided into Highveld, Middleveld, Lowveld, Bushveld.

Famous for: Gold and diamond mining region (Johannesburg = "City of Gold" in the Highveld).

Wildlife: Zebra, wildebeest, antelopes — big game safaris (Kruger National Park area).

Savanna Africa

Region: Most of Africa (the classic "African grassland"), also parts of South America, Australia, India (Indian savanna = the Terai and parts of the Deccan).

Climate: Tropical — two seasons: wet and dry. Tall grasses + scattered trees (acacia, baobab).

Famous for: The African savanna is home to the "Big Five" — lion, leopard, elephant, rhino, buffalo.

Not to confuse: Savanna = tropical, Prairies/Steppes/Pampas/Velds = temperate.

2 · The rest of the world (equally exam-worthy)

Llanos South America

Country: Venezuela (also eastern Colombia).

Climate: Tropical savanna — flooded in the rainy season, parched in the dry season.

Famous for: Huge cattle-ranching plains. The Llaneros are Venezuela's cowboys.

Campos South America

Country: Brazil (mainly the southern highlands).

Climate: Subtropical to tropical.

Famous for: Cattle, sheep grazing. Not the Amazon — that's rainforest.

Puszta Europe

Country: Hungary.

Climate: Temperate grassland on the Hungarian Plain (Pannonian Basin).

Famous for: A classic example of a European grassland — often asked as "Puszta is in which country?" Answer: Hungary.

Manchuria Asia

Country: China (northeast region — also historically called Manchuria).

Climate: Cold temperate.

Famous for: Soybean and maize cultivation; also the historic homeland of the Manchu people.

Downs Australia

Country: Australia (Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria).

Climate: Temperate grassland — mild, with reliable rainfall.

Famous for: Sheep and cattle grazing — Australia is one of the world's largest wool producers.

Canterbury Oceania

Country: New Zealand (Canterbury Plains, South Island).

Climate: Temperate, moist.

Famous for: Sheep farming, dairy, and lamb exports. The Canterbury Plains are New Zealand's most productive farmland.

4 · Memory tricks that save you

Trick 1 — The "P" family in South America.
"PAMPAS, LLANOS, CAMPOS"
• Pampas → Argentina (biggest, cattle + wheat)
• Llanos → Venezuela (cattle)
• Campos → Brazil (grazing)
Remember: all three are in South America. Not one of them is in Europe.
Trick 2 — The two "Steppes" of the world.
"Steppe = Russia; Manchuria = China"
Steppes cover the world's largest continuous grassland — from Ukraine all the way to Mongolia. Manchuria is China's own grassland, in the northeast corner.
Trick 3 — Africa has two names.
"S for Savanna = Spread across all Africa; V for Velds = Very south (South Africa)"
Savanna is tropical (has trees); Velds are temperate (mostly open grass).
Trick 4 — The two "P" + Europe one.
"PUSZTA = Hungary" — the only famous grassland in Europe proper. Remember "Puszta and Paprika" — both are Hungarian.
Trick 5 — Two "D" / "C" for Oceania.
"Downs are Down Under (Australia); Canterbury is in NEW Zealand"
Downs → Australia.
Canterbury → New Zealand (also a famous English city — don't mix the two).

6 · Temperate vs Tropical — the silent distinction

Feature Temperate Grassland Tropical Grassland (Savanna)
LocationMid-latitudes (30°–55° N/S)Near the equator (10°–30° N/S)
RainfallModerate, spread through the yearTwo seasons — wet + dry
TreesAlmost none — pure grassScattered trees (acacia, baobab)
Grass heightShort to mediumTall
ExamplesPrairies, Pampas, Steppes, Velds, Downs, Puszta, Campos, Llanos, Canterbury, ManchuriaAfrican Savanna
Exam tip: Almost all the world's grasslands are temperate — only the African Savanna is the classic tropical grassland. If a question says "tropical grassland", the answer is almost certainly Savanna.
