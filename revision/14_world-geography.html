World Geography: Deserts, Winds, Currents & Straits

Major deserts · tectonic plates · world winds · ocean currents · strategic straits — tap to reveal.

The four pillars of physical geography. This page combines four high-yield UPSC/BPSC topics:
  • Deserts — where rainfall is less than 25 cm/year.
  • Tectonic Plates — the moving slabs that make continents, mountains, and earthquakes.
  • Winds — local and global air movements that shape climate.
  • Ocean Currents & Straits — the "conveyor belt" of the seas and the choke points of global trade.

2 · Tectonic Plates — the moving earth

Major Plates (7)

Pacific · North American · South American · Eurasian · African · Indo-Australian · Antarctic

Minor Plates

Arabian · Caribbean · Cocos · Juan de Fuca · Nazca · Philippine · Anatolian · Scotia

The 7 Major Plates
  • Pacific Plate — largest plate; ring of fire.
  • North American Plate — covers most of North America and the western Atlantic.
  • South American Plate — covers South America and the western Atlantic.
  • Eurasian Plate — covers Europe and most of Asia.
  • African Plate — covers Africa and the eastern Atlantic.
  • Indo-Australian Plate — covers India, Australia, and the Indian Ocean.
  • Antarctic Plate — covers Antarctica and the surrounding ocean.
The Important Minor Plates
  • Arabian Plate — moving north, colliding with Eurasia (Zagros mountains).
  • Caribbean Plate — Central America and the Caribbean islands.
  • Cocos Plate — off the west coast of Central America (subducting under North America).
  • Juan de Fuca Plate — off the northwest coast of the USA (subducting under North America).
  • Nazca Plate — off the west coast of South America (subducting under South America, forming the Andes).
  • Philippine Plate — beneath the Philippines (subducting under the Eurasian plate).
  • Anatolian Plate — Turkey (squeezed between Eurasian and Arabian plates).
Boundary types — quick refresher:
• Divergent — plates move apart (Mid-Atlantic Ridge).
• Convergent — plates collide (Himalayas, Andes).
• Transform — plates slide past each other (San Andreas Fault).

3 · Major World Winds

Grouped by type — tap to reveal location and effect.

Hot & Dry Winds

Loo Hot India/Pakistan
Blows in summer (April–June) over northern plains of India and Pakistan. Very hot and dry — causes heat waves. West to east direction.
Foehn Hot Alps
Warm, dry wind on the leeward side of the Alps. Raises temperature suddenly. Similar to Chinook.
Chinook Hot Rockies
Warm, dry wind on the eastern slopes of the Rockies (USA/Canada). Causes rapid snowmelt — called the "Snow Eater".
Sirocco Hot Sahara
Hot, dry, dusty wind blowing from the Sahara towards the Mediterranean Sea. Brings sandstorms and haze.
Khamsin Hot Egypt
Hot, dry wind over Egypt. Raises temperature in spring. Named after the Arabic word for "fifty" (blows for ~50 days).
Harmattan Hot West Africa
Very hot, dry wind blowing from the northeast across West Africa. Carries dust from the Sahara, lowering visibility.
Brickfielder Hot Australia
Hot, dry wind in southern Australia. Raises temperature suddenly.
Zonda Hot Andes
Warm, dry wind in the Andes (Argentina). Causes sudden rise in temperature. The South American equivalent of Foehn.

Cold & Dry Winds

Mistral Cold France/Mediterranean
Cold, dry, strong wind blowing from the Alps towards the Mediterranean Sea. Blows in winter. Brings clear skies and cold days.
Bora Cold Adriatic
Cold, dry, gusty wind blowing from the mountains into the Adriatic Sea region (Italy, Croatia). Blows from land to sea. Can be very strong.
Blizzard Cold Antarctica
Extremely cold, strong wind in Antarctica. Causes severe snowstorms and "whiteout" conditions.
Buran Cold Central Asia
Very cold, dry wind in Central Asia and Russia. Causes snowstorms in winters. The Siberian equivalent of a blizzard.

Moist / Cool Winds

Pampero Argentina
Cool, moist wind blowing over the Pampas of Argentina and Uruguay. Brings sudden change in weather — often a welcome relief after heat.

4 · Global Ocean Currents — the conveyor belt

Warm currents (red arrows) flow from the equator towards the poles → bring mild weather.
Cold currents (blue arrows) flow from the poles towards the equator → create arid coastal deserts (fog, no rain).

Warm Currents

Gulf StreamNorth Atlantic — warms Europe
KuroshioNorth Pacific — warms Japan
Brazil CurrentSouth Atlantic — warms Brazil
E. Australian CurrentSouth Pacific — warms Australia

Cold Currents

California CurrentNorth Pacific — cools USA west coast
Peru (Humboldt)South Pacific — causes Atacama desert
BenguelaSouth Atlantic — causes Namib desert
Canary CurrentNorth Atlantic — cools NW Africa
W. Australian CurrentIndian Ocean — cools W Australia
OyashioNorth Pacific — cools Japan

The Five Major Gyres

GyreOceanKey Feature
North Atlantic GyreAtlanticContains the Gulf Stream
South Atlantic GyreAtlanticContains the Brazil & Benguela currents
North Pacific GyrePacificContains the Kuroshio & California currents
South Pacific GyrePacificContains the Peru & E. Australian currents
Indian Ocean GyreIndianContains the W. Australian current
Driving factors of ocean currents:
• Wind — trade winds, westerlies, polar easterlies.
• Earth's rotation (Coriolis effect) — deflects currents clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere, counter-clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere.
• Water density — temperature and salinity differences drive thermohaline circulation (the "Great Ocean Conveyor Belt").

5 · Major Straits of the World

Tap each strait to reveal what it connects and why it matters.

Strait of Malacca Strait
Connects the Indian Ocean with the Pacific Ocean. Between Malaysia and Sumatra (Indonesia). Handles ~25% of global seaborne trade. Critical for Asian energy imports (oil from the Middle East to China/Japan).
Strait of Magellan Strait
A maritime gateway in southern Chile. Connects the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans. Important for global shipping before the Panama Canal.
Bass Strait Strait
Separates Tasmania from mainland Australia. Direct passage between the Great Australian Bight and the Tasman Sea.
Strait of Hormuz Strait
Key chokepoint between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. Between Iran and Oman/UAE. Carries ~20% of global oil trade and ~25% of global oil consumption. The world's most important oil choke point.
Singapore Strait Strait
Lies between the Malacca Strait and the South China Sea. One of the world's busiest straits. Vital for the Malacca–Singapore route. Between Malaysia, Singapore, and Indonesia.
Bering Strait Strait
The only waterway between the Arctic and the Pacific Ocean. Separates Asia (Russia) and North America (Alaska, USA).
Strait of Gibraltar Strait
Connects the Atlantic Ocean to the Mediterranean Sea. Separates Europe (Spain) from Africa (Morocco).
Bosphorus Strait Strait
The narrowest strait for international navigation. Connects the Black Sea with the Sea of Marmara. Separates Europe from Asia (Turkey).

6 · Memory tricks that save you

Trick 1 — Deserts by continent:
"Sahara, Kalahari, Namib (Africa) · Arabian, Thar, Gobi (Asia) · Atacama, Patagonian (S. America)"
Remember: Africa has 3 famous ones, Asia has 3 famous ones, South America has 2.
Trick 2 — The 7 major plates:
"P-N-S-E-A-I-A" → Pacific, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian, Antarctic.
Mnemonic: "Please Notice Some Elephants Are In Africa".
Trick 3 — Winds by temperature:
Hot: Loo, Foehn, Chinook, Sirocco, Khamsin, Harmattan, Brickfielder, Zonda.
Cold: Mistral, Bora, Blizzard, Buran.
Moist: Pampero.
Mnemonic: "Loo Foehn Chinook Sirocco, Khamsin Harmattan — Hot and Dry so severe; Mistral Bora Blizzard Brickfielder, Pampero Zonda Buran — Cold winds we remember."
Trick 4 — Ocean currents (Warm vs Cold):
"Gulf Stream warms Europe; Peru cools South America; Benguela cools Africa; California cools USA."
Cold currents = coastal deserts (Atacama, Namib). Warm currents = mild climates (Europe).
Trick 5 — Straits and what they connect:
Malacca = Indian ↔ Pacific.
Gibraltar = Atlantic ↔ Mediterranean.
Hormuz = Persian Gulf ↔ Gulf of Oman.
Bering = Arctic ↔ Pacific.
Bosphorus = Black Sea ↔ Sea of Marmara.
Magellan = Atlantic ↔ Pacific (southern Chile).
