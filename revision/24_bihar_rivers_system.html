2 · North Bihar — the Himalayan giants

Snow and monsoon fed, perennial, silt-heavy and flood-prone. These are the rivers that make the news every August.

3 · South Bihar — the plateau streams

Rain-fed, seasonal, and born in the Chotanagpur plateau or the Kaimur range. They dry in May and flood in July.

6 · Memory tricks that save you

Six hooks — say them out loud once, and the list stops being a list.

North vs South, in two letters.

N-H = P · S-P = S

North + Himalaya = Perennial. South + Plateau = Seasonal. If it is snow-fed and floods, it is north; if it dries in May, it is south.

The Nepal set.

GGBK · ALL

Ghaghara, Gandak, Bagmati, Kosi — plus Adhwaa, Lakhandei, Lalbakeya. "Great Gandak Brings Kosi; All Lakes Leak."

The southern six.

S-P-K-C-B-A

Son, Punpun, Kiul, Chandan, Badua, Ajay — "Son Pours Killing Currents, Bad Animals." The rest (Phalgu, Sakri, Karmanasa, Morhar, Dardha) hang off these.

One river, three names.

GKS

Ghaghara = Karnali (Nepal) = Saryu (lower reach near Ayodhya). Same river — do not treat them as three.

The four traps.

M-A-F-B

Mahananda misses Bihar's Ganga (joins Padma in Bangladesh). Ajay avoids the Ganga (joins Hooghly). Falgu follows Punpun. Bagmati bypasses the Ganga (joins Kosi first).

The Kosi's westward walk.

112 km

After the mid-1950s breach the Kosi shifted roughly 112 km west. "Kosi Kare 112 km Ka Safar" — the single most repeated flood fact in Bihar exams.
