International Organisations — Part 1

World Bank · WTO · ILO · WEF · UN · plus the orgs every UPSC/BPSC paper quietly asks about.

How to use this page: every blue-accented card is closed by default. Tap it (or hit Reveal all) only after you have tried to recall the answer. Reading an open card teaches you nothing — guessing first is what makes it stick.

1 · The World Bank Group — five arms, one mission

Founded at Bretton Woods in 1944. Began operations June 1946. HQ: Washington DC. 189 member countries. India is a founder member.

IBRD 1944 Middle Income
International Bank for Reconstruction and Development. The original "World Bank". Lends to middle-income and creditworthy low-income countries at near-market rates. Started life funding post-WWII reconstruction (hence the name).
IDA 1960 Poorest
International Development Association. The "soft loan window". Gives concessional loans and grants to the world's poorest countries. Known as the Soft Loan Window of the World Bank — the single most-asked fact in this section.
IFC 1956 Private Sector
International Finance Corporation. Invests in and lends to the private sector in developing countries — no government guarantees required. Only arm of the group focused purely on private enterprise.
MIGA 1988 Insurance
Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency. Provides political-risk insurance and guarantees to cross-border investors — protects against expropriation, war, civil disturbance, breach of contract.
ICSID 1966 Disputes
International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes. A neutral forum for arbitration between foreign investors and host states. Based in Washington DC. Not a lender — a tribunal.
Watch out: The World Bank Group has 5 arms; the "World Bank" strictly means IBRD + IDA only. Examiners love this distinction.

2 · WTO — the referee of world trade

Created 1 January 1995 by the Marrakesh Agreement. Replaced GATT (which had run since 1948). HQ: Geneva, Switzerland. Not a UN body. India is a founding member.

General Council Highest Body
The highest decision-making body of the WTO, meeting regularly at Geneva. It also sits as the Dispute Settlement Body and the Trade Policy Review Body.
Ministerial Conference Every 2 Years
The WTO's supreme / main decision-making body — meets at least once every two years. It is where all major new trade agreements are negotiated. (Note: General Council = highest standing body; Ministerial Conference = highest plenary body.)
Secretariat Admin
Provides administrative and technical support to WTO bodies. Headed by the Director-General. Located in Geneva.
MC14 Recent
The 14th WTO Ministerial Conference was held in Cameroon. Key flashpoint: the attempt to renew the 26-year-old e-commerce moratorium on customs duties on digital trade — India opposed renewing it without a development outcome.
E-commerce Moratorium India Opposed
Since 1998 the WTO has not imposed customs duties on electronic transmissions. It has been rolled over again and again. Developing countries (led by India and South Africa) argue it deprives them of tariff revenue and policy space for digital industrialisation.
Dispute Settlement — Appellate Body crisis Key Issue
The WTO's two-tier dispute system: Panels → Appellate Body. Since 2019 the US has blocked appointments to the Appellate Body, leaving it non-functional. Members now use the MPIA (Multi-Party Interim Appeal Arbitration Arrangement) as a workaround — India is not a member of the MPIA.

Subsidy "Boxes" — the comparison you must remember

Other WTO agreements worth a card in your head

  • AoA — Agreement on Agriculture (market access, domestic support, export subsidies)
  • GATS — General Agreement on Trade in Services: Mode 1 cross-border, Mode 2 consumption abroad, Mode 3 commercial presence, Mode 4 movement of natural persons
  • TRIPS — Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights
  • TRIMS — Trade-Related Investment Measures
  • SPS — Sanitary and Phytosanitary Measures (food safety, animal/plant health)
  • TBT — Technical Barriers to Trade (standards, labelling, packaging)
  • Anti-Dumping / Countervailing Duties / Safeguards — the three trade-remedy tools

3 · ILO — Geneva's tripartite guardian of labour

Founded 1919 by the Treaty of Versailles. HQ: Geneva. Became a UN specialised agency in 1946. Nobel Peace Prize 1969. 187 member states. India is a founding member.

Tripartite Structure Unique Feature
The ILO is the only UN agency with a tripartite structure — every decision is taken jointly by three constituents: Governments + Employers + Workers. This is the single most-examined fact about the ILO.
Objectives Mandate
  • Promote and realise standards and fundamental labour rights
  • Create decent work opportunities for women and men
  • Enhance social protection and strengthen social dialogue
  • Eradicate forced labour, child labour and discrimination at work
8 Fundamental Conventions Core
  • Forced Labour: C29 (1930), C105 (1957)
  • Child Labour: C138 — Minimum Age (1973), C182 — Worst Forms (1999)
  • Equality / Non-discrimination: C100 — Equal Remuneration (1951), C111 — Discrimination (1958)
  • Freedom of Association & Collective Bargaining: C87 (1948), C98 (1949)
All eight are binding on ratifying states; India has ratified six (not C87 and C98).
ILO Declaration, 1998 Principles
Four non-negotiable principles, binding on all members even without ratification:
  1. Freedom of association & effective recognition of collective bargaining
  2. Elimination of all forms of forced or compulsory labour
  3. Effective abolition of child labour
  4. Elimination of discrimination in respect of employment and occupation
Key ILO Reports Reports
  • World Employment and Social Outlook (WESO)
  • Global Wage Report
  • World Social Protection Report
  • World of Work Report

4 · WEF — the Davos club

An international non-profit foundation, not a treaty body. HQ: Geneva, Switzerland. Founder: Klaus Schwab.

1971 — Founded Origin
Founded by Klaus Schwab as the European Management Forum — a non-profit bringing European business leaders together. Headquartered in Geneva.
1973 — The Turning Point Expansion
Global economic turmoil — the collapse of the Bretton Woods system and the first oil shock — forced the forum to widen its agenda from "management" to global economic and political issues. Membership was later opened to roughly 1,000 leading global companies.
1987 — Renamed Rename
Renamed from European Management Forum to World Economic Forum to reflect its genuinely global role.
2015 — Formal recognition Status
Formally recognised as an international organisation by the Swiss government — which is why it is now treated alongside treaty bodies in exam lists.
56th Annual Meeting — Davos Recent
Held in Davos, Switzerland. Theme: "A Spirit of Dialogue". The Annual Meeting is the WEF's flagship event; the venue is what gives the forum its everyday nickname — "Davos".

5 · The United Nations — six organs, one charter

Charter signed 26 June 1945 at San Francisco; came into force 24 October 1945 (UN Day). HQ: New York. 193 members.

UNGA — General Assembly Universal
The only UN organ with universal representation — all 193 members, one nation, one vote.
  • Deliberative, policy-making and representative organ
  • "Important questions" need a two-thirds majority; others need a simple majority
  • President elected each year, serves a one-year term
UNSC — Security Council 15 Members
15 members: 5 permanent (China, France, Russia, UK, USA — the P5, each with veto) and 10 non-permanent elected for two-year terms.
  • Elections held annually by secret ballot, requiring a two-thirds majority
  • Remains the only UN body whose decisions are binding on all members under the Charter
  • Non-permanent seats by region: Africa 3, Asia-Pacific 3, GRULAC 2, WEOG 2, Eastern Europe 1
UNSC — Recent Elected Members 2025–27
  • 2025–2026: Bahrain, Colombia, DR Congo, Latvia, Liberia
  • 2026–2027: Denmark, Greece, Pakistan, Panama, Somalia
Note: five seats turn over each year, so the council always has a mix of first-year and second-year members.
India's UNSC Terms India
India has been elected as a non-permanent member eight times: 1950–51 · 1967–68 · 1972–73 · 1977–78 · 1984–85 · 1991–92 · 2011–12 · 2021–22. India is a founder member of the UN (1945) and has long demanded permanent membership.
G4 & the Coffee Club Reform Blocs
  • G4: Brazil, Germany, India, Japan — demand permanent UNSC seats for themselves.
  • Coffee Club (Uniting for Consensus): formed in the 1990s, opposes expansion of permanent seats. Members include Italy, Pakistan, Mexico, Egypt, South Korea, Colombia, Canada and others.
The two blocs cancel each other out — which is why UNSC reform has stalled for three decades.
ECOSOC 54 Members
Economic and Social Council — principal body for coordination, policy review, policy dialogue and recommendations on economic, social and environmental issues. Established 1945. 54 members, elected by the General Assembly for overlapping three-year terms. India 2026–28 India was elected for the 2026–28 term.
ICJ — International Court of Justice The Hague
The principal judicial organ of the UN. Established 1945.
  • Seat: Peace Palace, The Hague (Netherlands)
  • 15 judges, elected for 9-year terms by UNGA and UNSC voting separately
  • No two judges may be nationals of the same state
  • Official languages: English and French
  • Advisory opinions are non-binding; judgments in contentious cases are binding on the parties
  • Regional composition: Africa 3, Latin America & Caribbean 2, Asia 3, WEOG 5, Eastern Europe 2
Trusteeship Council Dormant
Created to supervise trust territories. Suspended operations in 1994 after Palau (the last trust territory) became independent. Still exists on paper — a favourite trick question: "which UN principal organ is effectively inactive?"
Secretariat & Secretary-General Executive
Carries out the day-to-day work of the UN. Headed by the Secretary-General, appointed by the UNGA on the recommendation of the UNSC for a 5-year renewable term. Staff are drawn from all member states.
UNCTAT / Taliban Sanctions Committee 1973 / 2025
The 1988 Sanctions Committee (Taliban) was set up under UNSC Resolution 1267 (1999) and strengthened after the 9/11 attacks. In 2025, India chaired the Taliban Sanctions Committee, with France and Russia as vice-chairs.

7 · The exits & the solar club

Two big withdrawals and one India-led launch — all three are near-certain current-affairs questions.

UNESCO US Exit India 1946
  • HQ: Paris, France. Specialised agency of the UN.
  • The US announced its withdrawal from UNESCO, with the pull-out taking effect from December 2026.
  • India has been a member since 1946 — one of the founding members.
WHO US Exit 1948
  • Established 1948; HQ: Geneva, Switzerland; special adviser to ECOSOC.
  • The USA withdrew from the WHO in January 2026.
  • Before withdrawal, the US was the WHO's largest donor, providing 12–15% of total funding — the key reason this exit matters so much financially.
International Solar Alliance (ISA) India + France
  • Launched in 2015 by India and France at COP21 (Paris).
  • Headquarters: Gurugram, India — the first major international organisation HQ'd in India.
  • Motto/target: "One World, One Sun, One Grid"
  • Goal: mobilise USD 1,000 billion of investment in solar energy solutions by 2030.
  • Membership open to all UN members (widened from the original "sunshine countries" between the Tropics).

8 · Beyond Part 1 — the orgs UPSC/BPSC actually asks

Not in the original notes. Added because Prelims keeps pulling from this exact list.

IMF 1944 Bretton Woods Twin
International Monetary Fund. Founded at Bretton Woods, 1944. HQ: Washington DC.
  • Mandate: macroeconomic stability, exchange-rate stability, balance-of-payments support
  • Reports: World Economic Outlook, Global Financial Stability Report, Fiscal Monitor
  • Special Drawing Rights (SDR) basket: USD, EUR, CNY, JPY, GBP (Chinese yuan added 2016)
  • Quota-based voting; 16th General Review of Quotas agreed to realign shares — India is now the 8th-largest quota holder
NDB — New Development Bank BRICS India
Established by the BRICS nations at the Fortaleza Summit (2014). HQ: Shanghai, China. India is a founding member and first President (K. V. Kamath). Also called the "BRICS Bank".
AIIB — Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank 2016
Started operations 2016. HQ: Beijing, China. India is the second-largest shareholder (after China). Focus: infrastructure in Asia and beyond.
ADB — Asian Development Bank 1966
Founded 1966. HQ: Manila, Philippines. India is a founding member. Largest shareholders: Japan and the USA.
BRICS Expanded 2024
Originally Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa. Expanded from 2024 to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE (and Saudi Arabia invited). Focus: reform of global governance, de-dollarisation debate, NDB.
G20 1999 India 2023
Created 1999 after the Asian financial crisis. India held the 2023 Presidency and hosted the New Delhi Summit — the African Union was admitted as a permanent member at that summit. The 2026 Presidency is with the USA.
SCO — Shanghai Cooperation Organisation 2001
Founded 2001 (Shanghai Five + Uzbekistan). Permanent members: China, Russia, India, Pakistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, Iran (2023), Belarus (2024). HQ: Beijing. India and Pakistan joined in 2017.
QUAD Indo-Pacific
Quadrilateral Security Dialogue — India, USA, Japan, Australia. Revived in 2017; elevated to leader-level summits from 2021. Focus: free and open Indo-Pacific, maritime security, critical technologies, resilient supply chains.
FATF 1989
Financial Action Task Force. Founded 1989 by the G7. HQ: Paris. Sets global standards on anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-terrorist financing (CFT). Maintains the Grey List and Black List. India is a member.
SAARC vs BIMSTEC India's Neighbourhood
  • SAARC — 1985, Dhaka. 8 members. HQ: Kathmandu. Largely paralysed by India–Pakistan friction; no summit since 2014.
  • BIMSTEC — 1997, Bangkok. 7 members (5 South Asian + Myanmar & Thailand). Permanent Secretariat: Dhaka. India's preferred regional vehicle under the "Neighbourhood First" and "Act East" policies.
OPEC 1960
Organisation of the Petroleum Exporting Countries. Founded 1960 at the Baghdad Conference. HQ: Vienna, Austria. "OPEC+" includes non-OPEC producers like Russia. Founded by Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia and Venezuela.
OECD 1961
Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development. Formed 1961 (successor to the OEEC, which ran the Marshall Plan). HQ: Paris. Club of mostly high-income economies. India is not a member.
Commonwealth 1949
Modern Commonwealth dates from the London Declaration, 1949, which allowed India to remain a member as a republic. 56 members. Secretariat: London. Head: the British monarch (ceremonial, not the head of government). India is a founding member.

9 · Memory tricks that save you

The five World Bank arms — by year. "44 the Bank, 56 the Firms, 60 the Aid, 66 the Courts, 88 the Guards"
1944 IBRD → 1956 IFC → 1960 IDA → 1966 ICSID → 1988 MIGA. Read the years in ascending order and the names fall into place, because each arm was added as a gap appeared: reconstruction, then private capital, then the poorest countries, then dispute settlement, then political risk.
World Bank vs IMF — one line. "Bank Builds, Fund Fixes"
The Bank lends for long-term development; the Fund fixes short-term balance-of-payments crises. Both are 1944 Bretton Woods twins, both sit in Washington DC.
ILO's eight core conventions — four pairs. "Force · Child · Equal · Free"
Then recall each pair by proximity of number: Forced = 29 & 105 · Child = 138 & 182 · Equal = 100 & 111 · Free = 87 & 98. India has ratified six — the two missing are exactly the freedom-of-association pair 87 & 98.
The six principal UN organs — in Charter order. "Green Snakes Eat Icy Sweet Things"
GA → SC → ECOSOC → ICJ → Secretariat → Trusteeship Council. (Trusteeship last because it is the only one that is effectively dead — suspended 1994.)
WTO subsidy boxes — the traffic-light trick. "Amber slow down, Blue stay put, Green go free"
Amber = trade-distorting, must be reduced. Blue = production-limiting, exempt. Green = non-distorting, no limit at all. Once you see the colours as a traffic signal, you never mix up Blue and Green again.
WEF's three dates — a 16-year ladder. "71 born, 73 grew up, 87 got its real name, 2015 got its licence"
1971 European Management Forum → 1973 agenda widens after Bretton Woods collapse → 1987 renamed World Economic Forum → 2015 recognised by Switzerland as an international organisation.

10 · Rapid Fire

Cover the whole page. Answer out loud before you tap.

1. In which year and at which conference was the IBRD founded?

1944, at the Bretton Woods Conference (USA). Operations began June 1946.

2. Which World Bank arm provides concessional loans to the poorest countries, and in which year was it set up?

IDA — International Development Association, 1960. Nicknamed the "Soft Loan Window".

3. Which two World Bank Group arms were created in 1956 and 1988, and what do they do?

1956 — IFC (financing the private sector). 1988 — MIGA (political-risk insurance and guarantees).

4. When did the WTO come into being, and what did it replace?

1 January 1995, following the Marrakesh Agreement. It replaced GATT, which had governed world trade since 1948.

5. Is the WTO a UN body? Where is it headquartered?

No — the WTO is not a UN member or agency. HQ: Geneva, Switzerland. India is a founding member.

6. Name the WTO's highest decision-making body and its main plenary decision-making body.

Highest standing body — General Council. Main plenary body — Ministerial Conference, which meets at least once every two years.

7. Where was MC14 held, and which 26-year-old moratorium was at stake?

Cameroon. The e-commerce moratorium on customs duties on electronic transmissions — India opposed its renewal.

8. Which WTO organ is currently dysfunctional, and why?

The Appellate Body — the US has blocked appointments since 2019. Members now use the MPIA as an interim workaround; India is not a party to it.

9. In which year was the ILO founded, by which treaty, and where is it headquartered?

1919, by the Treaty of Versailles. HQ: Geneva, Switzerland. India is a founding member.

10. What is the ILO's unique structural feature, and in which year did it win the Nobel Peace Prize?

A tripartite structure — governments, employers and workers all participate. Nobel Peace Prize: 1969. It became a UN specialised agency in 1946.

11. Name the four themes of the ILO's eight fundamental conventions — and which two India has NOT ratified.

Forced labour, child labour, equality/non-discrimination, freedom of association & collective bargaining. India has not ratified C87 (Freedom of Association) and C98 (Right to Organise and Collective Bargaining).

12. Who founded the WEF, in which year, and what was it originally called?

Klaus Schwab, 1971. Originally the European Management Forum; renamed World Economic Forum in 1987.

13. Which 1973 event broadened the WEF's agenda, and what was the theme of the 56th Annual Meeting?

The collapse of the Bretton Woods system (plus the oil shock) forced a global agenda. 56th Annual Meeting theme: "A Spirit of Dialogue", at Davos.

14. How many members does the UNSC have, how many are permanent, and who are they?

15 total — 5 permanent (China, France, Russia, UK, USA — the P5 with veto) and 10 non-permanent, elected for two-year terms by secret ballot requiring a two-thirds majority.

15. Name the UNSC non-permanent members for 2025–26 and 2026–27.

2025–26: Bahrain, Colombia, DR Congo, Latvia, Liberia. 2026–27: Denmark, Greece, Pakistan, Panama, Somalia.

16. What is the G4, and what is the Coffee Club?

G4 = Brazil, Germany, India, Japan — want permanent UNSC seats. Coffee Club (Uniting for Consensus) = Italy, Pakistan, Mexico, Egypt, South Korea and others — oppose creating new permanent seats.

17. Which is the only UN organ with universal representation, and what majority do "important questions" need?

The UN General Assembly. Important questions need a two-thirds majority; other questions a simple majority. One nation, one vote.

18. Where does the ICJ sit, how many judges does it have, and what is their term?

Peace Palace, The Hague (Netherlands). 15 judges, 9-year terms, elected by UNGA and UNSC voting separately. Official languages: English and French. Advisory opinions are non-binding.

19. Which UN principal organ is effectively inactive, and since when?

The Trusteeship Council — suspended operations in 1994 after Palau, the last trust territory, became independent.

20. Which two countries launched the International Solar Alliance, when, and where is its HQ?

India and France, launched in 2015 at COP21 (Paris). HQ: Gurugram, India. Target: USD 1,000 billion of solar investment by 2030 under "One World, One Sun, One Grid".

21. Which country withdrew from UNESCO and which from the WHO, and when do those exits take effect?

The USA announced withdrawal from UNESCO, effective December 2026; and withdrew from the WHO in January 2026. The US was the WHO's largest donor at 12–15% of total funding.

22. Where are the IMF, NDB, AIIB and ADB headquartered?

IMF — Washington DC (1944, Bretton Woods). NDB — Shanghai (BRICS bank, 2014 Fortaleza Summit). AIIB — Beijing (2016). ADB — Manila (1966).
