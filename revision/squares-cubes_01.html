Patterns Worth Knowing

  • Squares are the running sum of odd numbers: 1 + 3 + 5 + … + (2n − 1) = n².
  • Gap between consecutive squares: n² − (n−1)² = 2n − 1 — always an odd number.
  • Gap between consecutive cubes: n³ − (n−1)³ = 3n² − 3n + 1.
  • A perfect square never ends in 2, 3, 7 or 8.
  • Digital root of any square is always 1, 4, 7 or 9.
  • A cube can end in any digit. Ends-in pattern of the base → cube: 0→0, 1→1, 4→4, 5→5, 6→6, 9→9 (same digit), while 2↔8 and 3↔7 swap.
  • Scaling by 10: 20² = 400 (×100), 20³ = 8000 (×1000). Reuse a known value and add zeros.

Fast Methods

Square of any number:   n² = (n − a)(n + a) + a²

  17² = 14 × 20 + 9   = 280 + 9    = 289      (a = 3)
  35² = 30 × 40 + 25  = 1200 + 25  = 1225     (a = 5)

Cube: reuse the square you already know — n³ = n × n²

  35³ = 35 × 1225 = 42,875
  24³ = 24 × 576  = 13,824
