Social & Religious Reform Movements in India

1814 → 1949. The Bengal renaissance, the Maharashtra anti-caste stream, Punjab's revivalism, the South Indian revolution, and the pan-India universalists.

How to use this page: every card is closed by default. Tap it — or hit Reveal all — only after you have tried to recall the year, the leader, and the one-line objective. Reading an open card teaches you nothing. Guessing first is what makes it stick.
Four streams, one paper. These movements are always asked in one of four ways: (1) Match year → movement → leader; (2) which region; (3) which movement was against caste vs. against sati vs. for women's education; (4) chronological order. Every section below is built so you can answer all four.

1 · Bengal — the Brahmo story & its children

Calcutta, 1814–1875. The starting point of every reform-movement question in Prelims.

Atmiya Sabha Bengal 1814
Leader: Raja Ram Mohan Roy Objective: Early reform platform — criticised sati and idol worship; emphasised belief in one God. Also called the "Friend's Association" — a weekly meeting of like-minded men. Significance: The first organised reform body of the Bengal Renaissance. It is the seed from which Brahmo Samaj grows.
Brahmo Samaj Bengal 1828
Leader: Raja Ram Mohan Roy Objective: Advocated rational thought and worked to eliminate social evils — especially caste discrimination and idol worship. The big win: Ram Mohan's campaign led to the abolition of Sati by Lord William Bentinck in 1829 (Regulation XVII).
Dharma Sabha Bengal 1830
Leader: Radhakant Deb Objective: The orthodox reaction — founded to counter the Brahmo Samaj and defend traditional Hindu practice. Interestingly, it still supported Western education for women. Exam angle: This is the classic "which body opposed the Brahmo Samaj" answer. Radhakant Deb was also a founder of the Hindu College (later Presidency College).
Tattvabodhini Sabha Bengal 1839
Leader: Debendranath Tagore (father of Rabindranath) Objective: Spread Brahmo Samaj teachings and drive intellectual reform in Bengal. Ran a journal called Tattvabodhini Patrika. Significance: Revived the Brahmo movement after Ram Mohan's death (1833) — Debendranath is the reason the Samaj survived.
Deva Samaj Bengal 1867
Leader: Shiv Narain Agnihotri Objective: Focused on ethical living, discouraged caste barriers, and inspired spiritual awakening. Quick distinction: A Brahmo offshoot with a strong ethical emphasis — not to be confused with Dev Samaj of Shiv Narayan Agnihotri in Lahore (1887), which many textbooks treat separately. Same founder, two spellings — Prelims has played with this.
Brahmo Samaj of India Bengal 1866 Added
Leader: Keshab Chandra Sen (after the split from Debendranath Tagore's Adi Brahmo Samaj) Objective: More radical — campaigned for inter-caste marriage, widow remarriage, and women's education. Keshab's daughter's inter-caste marriage in 1878 caused another split. Why it matters: Paves the way for the Sadharan Brahmo Samaj (1878), founded by Anand Mohan Bose and Sivanath Sastri — the most progressive wing.

2 · Maharashtra — the anti-caste engine & the service tradition

Pune and Bombay, 1849–1914. Two distinct streams here — caste resistance (Phule, Patil) and national service (Gokhale, Ranade, Kunzru).

Paramhans Mandali Maharashtra 1849
Leaders: Dadoba Pandurang, Bal Shastri Jambhekar Objective: Worship of a single deity, opposition to social hierarchies, and support for reforms like widow remarriage. Published the journal Darpan. Placement: The earliest reform body in Maharashtra — the local counterpart to Bengal's Atmiya Sabha.
Satyashodhak Samaj Maharashtra 1873
Leader: Jyotiba Phule (with Savitribai Phule) Objective: Resisted Brahminical supremacy; empowered lower castes through education.
  • Established schools for marginalised groups — Savitribai ran the first girls' school (1848, Bhide Wada, Pune).
  • Wrote Ghulamgiri ("Slavery") — dedicated to the African-American struggle, comparing caste to slavery.
  • Advocated social equality and opposed caste-based restrictions.
Exam keyword: Ghulamgiri + Satyashodhak Samaj + Jyotiba Phule = a permanent Prelims combo.
Bahujan Samaj Maharashtra 1910
Leader: Mukundrao Patil Objective: Worked against exploitation by Brahmin landlords and merchants; empowered lower-caste communities and promoted socio-economic advancement. Placement: A Maharashtra continuation of the Phule stream — not to be confused with Kanshi Ram's Bahujan Samaj Party (1984), which is a political party.
Indian National Social Conference Maharashtra 1887 Corrected
Actual founders: M. G. Ranade and Raghunath Rao — not M. K. Gandhi. (The note you shared has this wrong; Gandhi was only 18 in 1887 and still in London.) Objective: Held its first session in Madras alongside the Indian National Congress. Focused on social reform within the nationalist platform — caste, widow remarriage, women's education. Exam angle: The Social Conference was attached to the Congress sessions — that linkage itself is the question.
Deccan Education Society Maharashtra 1884 Corrected
Actual founders: Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Gopal Ganesh Agarkar, Mahadev Namjoshi, Vishnushastri Chiplunkar. Not M. K. Gandhi — this is a common error in the note you shared. Objective: Spread modern education; also addressed social issues like polygamy, caste-based marriage restrictions, and encouraged inter-caste marriage. Institution it created: Fergusson College, Pune (1885) — still one of India's leading colleges.
Nishkam Karma Math Maharashtra 1910
Leader: Bal Gangadhar Tilak (Lokmanya Tilak) Objective: Social service without expectation of reward — the karma yoga of the Gita applied to national regeneration. Tilak's answer to the moderate style of service politics.
Servants of India Society Maharashtra 1905
Leader: Gopal Krishna Gokhale Objective: Train "national missionaries" for the service of India — famine relief, education of the depressed classes, tribal welfare, and spreading political awareness. Interesting: On Gokhale's death in 1915, Gandhi — who regarded Gokhale as his political guru — was made the Society's first "life member"? No — the Society's leadership passed to V. S. Srinivasa Sastri. Gandhi was not its head.
Poona Seva Sadan Maharashtra 1909 Corrected
Leaders: Ramabai Ranade and G. K. Devadhar (the note calls him "DK Devdhar" — usually written G.K. Devadhar). Objective: Channelled education for widows and advocated selfless service. Ran a hostel, a training school, and literacy classes for women.
Seva Samiti Maharashtra 1914
Leader: Pandit Hridayanath Kunzru Objective: Community welfare, rehabilitation, and criminal justice reform. Famous for organising volunteers during the Kumbh Mela for sanitation, order, and lost-and-found services — the origin of the model that the Seva Dal later borrowed.
Prarthana Samaj Maharashtra 1867 Added
Leaders: Atmaram Pandurang (founder), with M. G. Ranade, R. G. Bhandarkar, and N. G. Chandavarkar. Inspiration: Keshab Chandra Sen's visit to Bombay in 1864 led to its formation — so it is a Brahmo-faith body, but it went further on caste and women. Programme: Abolition of caste, widow remarriage, women's education, and a reformist reading of the Hindu scriptures. Ran the journal Subodh Patrika. Why UPSC loves it: "Which reform body did M. G. Ranade belong to?" — Prarthana Samaj is almost always the answer.
Depressed Classes Institute & Samaj Samata Sangh Maharashtra 1924 / 1927 Added
Leader: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar
  • Depressed Classes Institute (Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha) — 1924: the first organisation founded by Ambedkar; for the education and welfare of the depressed classes.
  • Samaj Samata Sangh — 1927: for social equality; directly after the Mahad Satyagraha (1927) where Ambedkar led Dalits to drink water from the Chavdar tank.
Exam sequence: Phule (1873) → Ambedkar (1924, 1927). Always in that order.

3 · Punjab & the Northwest Frontier — revivalism and resistance

Sikh revival, a syncretic sect, and one of the greatest non-violent movements of the freedom struggle.

Namdhari / Kuka Movement Punjab 1841 / 1857
Leaders: Bhai Balak Singh (founder), Baba Ram Singh (organiser) Objective: Revived Sikh principles, opposed caste-based discrimination, and raised voices against British rule.
  • Called "Kuka" because of the shriek-like chanting during prayer — the nickname stuck.
  • Opposed the British, the cow slaughter, and the caste hierarchy inside Sikh society.
  • 1857 is the usual founding date (Hazro, Balak Singh); some sources use 1841. Safe answer: mid-19th century, Punjab, Baba Ram Singh.
Radhaswami Movement Punjab 1861
Leader: Tulsi Ram, also known as Shiv Dayal Saheb (Swamiji Maharaj) Objective: Devotion to a living guru, simple living, and the unity of all religions. Emerged from the Sant Mat tradition of the Punjab. Also remember: The sect's spiritual headquarters is at Dayalbagh, Agra — where the Radhasoami Satsang has its main centre.
Khudai Khidmatgar Movement NWFP 1929 Added
Leader: Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan — the "Frontier Gandhi" Objective: A non-violent "Servants of God" corps of Pashtun volunteers, drawn from the Qissa Khwani Bazaar tradition. Directly linked to the freedom struggle in the NWFP (now Khyber Pakhtunkhwa). Exam keyword: "Frontier Gandhi" + Khudai Khidmatgar + red-shirt volunteers = one precise question, almost every year.
Singh Sabha Movement Punjab 1873 Added
Founded at: Amritsar (first Singh Sabha); a second was founded at Lahore in 1879. Objective: Sikh religious and educational revival — counter Christian missionary activity, restore Sikh scriptures and institutions (Khalsa College, Amritsar), and reform Sikh social practice.

4 · Parsi reform — the Mazdayasnan stream

Rahnumai Mazdayasan Sabha Parsi 1851
Leaders: S. S. Bengali, Naoroji Furdonji, and J. B. Nacha (per your note — also associated with Dadabhai Naoroji and K. R. Cama in most standard textbooks) Objective: Promoted Zoroastrian principles, reformation within Parsi society, and modern education. Context: The Parsi community in Bombay was early to Western education and produced the first generation of Indian businessmen and nationalists. This Sabha is the intellectual root of that.
Ahmadiyya Movement Muslim reform 1889 Added
Leader: Mirza Ghulam Ahmad (Qadian, Punjab) Objective: A reformist current within Islam — emphasised a liberal, individual, and rational approach; built its own schools and missionary activity.
Aligarh Movement Muslim reform 1875 Added
Leader: Sir Syed Ahmad Khan Objective: Modern education for Muslims after 1857 — Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College, Aligarh (1875), which became Aligarh Muslim University (1920). Exam keyword: "Which reformer urged Muslims to take to English education?" — Sir Syed Ahmad Khan. He also wrote Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq.

5 · Pan-India universalists — Arya, Theosophy, Ramakrishna

Three movements whose reach extended beyond any one region — and which UPSC treats as a single cluster.

Arya Samaj Pan-India 1875
Leader: Swami Dayanand Saraswati Founded at: Bombay (10 April 1875) — later headquartered in Lahore. Objective:
  • Return to Vedic values — "Back to the Vedas"
  • Fought for gender equality; opposed child marriage; supported widow remarriage and women's education
  • Led Shuddhi (reconversion) — bringing converts back into the Hindu fold
  • Criticised idol worship, caste by birth, and untouchability
Famous work: Satyarth Prakash. The Samaj also built the D.A.V. (Dayanand Anglo-Vedic) school network.
Theosophical Society Pan-India 1875
Founders: Madame H. P. Blavatsky and Colonel H. S. Olcott (New York, 1875) Objective: Revived global interest in Indian philosophies and spiritual wisdom; promoted the unity of religions, education for the masses, and humanitarian service. India connection: Headquarters moved to Adyar, Madras (Chennai) in 1882. Annie Besant later led the Society and entered Indian politics from that platform. Exam favourite: "Where is the Theosophical Society's headquarters?" — Adyar, Chennai. Not New York, not London.
Ramakrishna Mission Pan-India 1897
Leader: Swami Vivekananda (disciple of Sri Ramakrishna Paramhansa) Founded at: Belur Math, near Calcutta. Vivekananda had already electrified the Parliament of Religions, Chicago (1893) before founding the Mission. Objective: Social service as practical Vedanta — hospitals, schools, relief work; "Atmano Mokshartham Jagaddhitaya Cha" (for one's own liberation and the welfare of the world).
Veda Samaj / Brahmo Samaj of South India South 1864 Added
Leader: K. Sridharalu Naidu (founded 1864, Madras) Objective: The Brahmo Samaj carried to the South — monotheism, opposition to caste, and reform of Hindu practice. Renamed the Brahmo Samaj of South India in 1871.

6 · South India — the anti-caste revolution

Kerala and Tamil Nadu, 1902–1949. The heaviest part of the syllabus for BPSC's South India questions.

SNDP Movement Kerala 1902–03
Full name: Shri Narayana Dharma Paripalana Yogam Leader: Shri Narayana Guru Objective: Promoted equality and rejected caste hierarchy. Spearheaded the Aravipuram Movement. Famous slogan: "One caste, one religion, one God for man" — the line that defines the entire movement. Also: Advocated unrestricted temple access for lower castes; installed the famous Shiva idol at Aruvippuram (1888).
Temple Entry Movement Kerala 1920s–1936
Leaders: T. K. Madhavan, N. Kumaran Asan, Shri Narayana Guru Objective: Campaigned for the right of lower castes to enter temples. Played a crucial role in dismantling caste-based discrimination in Kerala's temples. Two key episodes:
  • Vaikom Satyagraha (1924–25) — led by T. K. Madhavan; supported by Gandhi and Periyar. The road-blockade that lasted 20 months.
  • Temple Entry Proclamation (1936) — issued by the Maharaja of Travancore, Chithira Thirunal Balarama Varma. The legal breakthrough.
Self Respect Movement Tamil Nadu 1925
Leader: E. V. Ramaswami Naicker — "Periyar" (the Great One) Objective: Advocated a society free of caste-based oppression; promoted rational thought, self-respect, and social equality, rejecting Brahminical dominance. Methods: Anti-theism, "self-respect marriages" (without priests or rituals), rationalist journals (Kudi Arasu). Exam keyword: "Who started the Self Respect Movement?" — Periyar, 1925. He was also associated with the Justice Party (founded 1917).
Dravidar Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK) Tamil Nadu 1949
Leader: C. N. Annadurai (Anna) Objective: Worked to uplift Harijan communities and integrate them into society; emphasised Dravidian identity, social justice, and non-Brahmin rights. Origin: A split from Periyar's Dravidar Kazhagam in 1949 — Annadurai took the political wing, Periyar kept the social-reform wing.
Harijan Sevak Sangh Pan-India 1932
Leader: M. K. Gandhi Founded: 1932, after the Poona Pact — originally called the All India Anti-Untouchability League, renamed Harijan Sevak Sangh. Objective: Eradicate untouchability and caste discrimination; encourage inclusive temple access, simplified marriages without rituals, and uphold the dignity of marginalised groups. Funding fact: Gandhi insisted it be funded entirely by non-Harijans — it was meant to be a movement of the conscience of the upper castes, not a Harijan self-help body. Ambedkar criticised this design.

7 · Women's movements — the quiet half of the reform story

1897–1917. UPSC increasingly asks these as a separate cluster.

Bharat Stree Mandal Women 1910
Leader: Sarala Devi Chaudhurani Objective: Empowering women through education and social reform — one of the earliest all-India women's organisations. Ran a journal Bharati.
Indian Women's Association Women 1917
Leader: Annie Besant Objective: Pioneered women's rights in India, encouraging education and political participation. Context: The same year (1917) the Women's Indian Association deputation met Montagu — the first formal Indian women's political demand for the vote. Besant was the driving force behind both.
Arya Mahila Samaj Women 1882 Added
Founder: Pandita Ramabai Saraswati (Pune, 1882) Objective: Education and welfare of Indian women, especially widows. Her Sharda Sadan (1889, Bombay) gave shelter to widows and taught them self-reliance. Exam keyword: "Who founded the Arya Mahila Samaj?" — Pandita Ramabai, 1882.

8 · What was missing — a UPSC/BPSC add-on pack

Movements not in your notes, but which appear in the same Prelims questions. Skim, don't memorise.

Young Bengal Movement Bengal 1826–31
Leader: Henry Vivian Derozio (a Portuguese-Indian teacher at Hindu College) Objective: Radical rationalism, free thought, and social criticism. Derozio's students — the "Derozians" — attacked idol worship, caste, and the subjection of women.
Wahabi Movement Muslim revival 1820s
Leader: Syed Ahmad Barelvi (of Rai Bareilly, later settled at Patna) Objective: Purity of Islamic practice and resistance to British rule; the "Wahhabi" label was used by the British loosely. Strong in Bihar and the North-West Frontier.
Faraizi Movement Bengal 1818
Leader: Haji Shariatullah (later Dudu Miyan) Objective: Islamic revival in Bengal, and defence of peasantry against Hindu landlords and indigo planters. Merged religion and agrarian resistance.
Sadharan Brahmo Samaj Bengal 1878
Founders: Anand Mohan Bose, Sivanath Sastri, and others — after breaking from Keshab Chandra Sen over his autocratic style and the marriage of his under-age daughter. Objective: The most progressive wing of the Brahmo movement — openly committed to caste abolition, women's education, and democratic functioning.
Justice Party Tamil Nadu 1917
Founders: T. M. Nair, P. Thyagaraya Chetty, and others (Madras) Objective: Non-Brahmin political representation in the Madras Presidency. The political twin of the Self Respect Movement — Periyar later took it over and renamed it the Dravidar Kazhagam (1944).

9 · Memory tricks that save you

The four Bengals, in order. "Atmiya 14, Brahmo 28, Dharma 30, Tattva 39"
Read them as a story: 1814 Ram Mohan starts a small friend-circle → 1828 he turns it into a formal Samaj → 1830 the orthodox hit back with Dharma Sabha → 1839 Debendranath revives the Samaj as Tattvabodhini. Four dates, four roles, one arc.
Phule vs Ambedkar — two Maharashtra milestones. "73 Phule founds, 24 Ambedkar founds"
Satyashodhak Samaj (1873) is Phule. Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924) is Ambedkar. Both are Maharashtra, both about caste, both 51 years apart. The gap is deliberate — remember it as the "Phule → Ambedkar handover".
Two 1875s — the great twin year. "Vedic and Vedantic, both born in '75"
Arya Samaj (Dayanand Saraswati, Bombay) is the Vedic revival — back to the Vedas, Shuddhi, D.A.V. schools. Theosophical Society (Blavatsky + Olcott, New York → Adyar) is the Vedantic revival — Hindu philosophy to the world, Adyar HQ. Same year, opposite directions.
The South Indian anti-caste ladder. "Aruvippuram 88 → SNDP 03 → Vaikom 24 → Temple Entry 36 → Self Respect 25 → DMK 49"
Narayana Guru's idol installation (1888) → the organisation (1902–03) → the street satyagraha (1924) → the legal victory (1936) → Periyar's rationalist movement (1925) → Annadurai's political party (1949). Read it as spiritual → organisational → agitational → legal → political.
Two 1870s Punjab-origin, three Punjabi movements — sorted by what they revive. "Namdhari = Sikh rites, Radhaswami = Guru bhakti, Singh Sabha = Sikh institutions"
Namdhari (mid-19th c.) — revival of Sikh practice + anti-British. Radhaswami (1861) — guru devotion + unity of religions. Singh Sabha (1873) — Sikh education + defence against Christian missions. Same province, different jobs.
Gokhale vs Gandhi — who founded what. "Gokhale serves, Gandhi untouches, Ranade conferences"
Gokhale → Servants of India Society (1905). Gandhi → Harijan Sevak Sangh (1932). Ranade + Raghunath Rao → Indian National Social Conference (1887). If a question puts Gandhi and 1887 together, it is a trap.
Three corrections to the notes you shared: (1) Indian National Social Conference (1887) — founders were M. G. Ranade and Raghunath Rao, not Gandhi. (2) Deccan Education Society (1884) — founders were Tilak, Agarkar, Namjoshi, Chiplunkar, not Gandhi. (3) Poona Seva Sadan (1909) — founded by Ramabai Ranade and G. K. Devadhar (not "DK Devdhar"). Get these right; Prelims has set traps on all three.

10 · Rapid Fire

Cover the whole page. Answer out loud before you tap.

1. Which was the earliest reform body of the Bengal Renaissance, and who founded it?

Atmiya Sabha, 1814, Raja Ram Mohan Roy — Calcutta.

2. Which reform body was founded to oppose the Brahmo Samaj, by whom, and in which year?

Dharma Sabha, 1830, by Radhakant Deb. Interestingly, it still supported Western education for women.

3. Who founded the Tattvabodhini Sabha, in which year, and to what purpose?

Debendranath Tagore, 1839 — to spread Brahmo Samaj teachings and drive intellectual reform in Bengal. Ran the Tattvabodhini Patrika.

4. Which reformer's campaign led to the abolition of Sati, and in which year?

Raja Ram Mohan Roy's campaign led to the abolition of Sati by Lord William Bentinck in 1829 (Regulation XVII).

5. Who founded the Paramhans Mandali, when, and what journal did it run?

Dadoba Pandurang and Bal Shastri Jambhekar, 1849, Maharashtra. Journal: Darpan.

6. In which year was the Satyashodhak Samaj founded, by whom, and which book is associated with it?

1873, Jyotiba Phule. Book: Ghulamgiri ("Slavery"). Savitribai Phule ran the first girls' school (1848, Pune).

7. Which movement did Shri Narayana Guru lead, and what was its full name?

The SNDP Movement — Shri Narayana Dharma Paripalana Yogam, founded 1902–03. He spearheaded the Aravipuram Movement, and his slogan was "One caste, one religion, one God for man".

8. What was the Vaikom Satyagraha, when did it happen, and who led it?

A temple-entry / road-access movement in Kerala, 1924–25, led by T. K. Madhavan with support from Gandhi and Periyar. It is the most famous episode of the Temple Entry Movement.

9. Who issued the Temple Entry Proclamation, in which year, and what did it do?

The Maharaja of Travancore, Chithira Thirunal Balarama Varma, in 1936 — opening Hindu temples in Travancore to all castes.

10. Who started the Self Respect Movement, in which year, and what was his nickname?

E. V. Ramaswami Naicker — Periyar — in 1925, in Tamil Nadu. He rejected Brahminical dominance and promoted rationalism and self-respect marriages.

11. When and by whom was the DMK founded, and from which party did it split?

C. N. Annadurai, 1949 — a split from Periyar's Dravidar Kazhagam. Emphasised Dravidian identity, social justice, and non-Brahmin rights.

12. When was the Harijan Sevak Sangh founded, by whom, and what was it originally called?

1932, by M. K. Gandhi — originally the All India Anti-Untouchability League, renamed after the Poona Pact. Gandhi insisted it be funded entirely by non-Harijans.

13. Who founded the Arya Samaj, in which year, and at which place?

Swami Dayanand Saraswati, 10 April 1875, Bombay. Later HQ shifted to Lahore. Famous work: Satyarth Prakash. Led Shuddhi and founded D.A.V. schools.

14. Who founded the Theosophical Society, in which year, and where is its Indian headquarters?

Madame H. P. Blavatsky and Colonel H. S. Olcott, 1875 (New York). Indian HQ shifted to Adyar, Madras (Chennai) in 1882.

15. Who founded the Ramakrishna Mission, in which year, and where?

Swami Vivekananda, 1897, at Belur Math near Calcutta — four years after his address at the Parliament of Religions, Chicago (1893).

16. Who founded the Servants of India Society, in which year, and for what purpose?

Gopal Krishna Gokhale, 1905 — to train "national missionaries" for famine relief, education of the depressed classes, tribal welfare, and political awareness.

17. Who actually founded the Indian National Social Conference in 1887?

M. G. Ranade and Raghunath Rao — not Gandhi. It held its first session in Madras alongside the Indian National Congress.

18. Who founded the Deccan Education Society, in which year, and which famous college did it create?

Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Gopal Ganesh Agarkar, Mahadev Namjoshi, and Vishnushastri Chiplunkar — 1884, Pune. It created Fergusson College (1885).

19. Who founded the Radhaswami Movement, in which year, and under what other name is he known?

Tulsi Ram, also known as Shiv Dayal Saheb, 1861, Punjab. The main centre is Dayalbagh, Agra.

20. Which movement was called the Kuka Movement, and who led it?

The Namdhari Movement — founded by Bhai Balak Singh (mid-19th century; 1857 in most sources), organised by Baba Ram Singh. Called "Kuka" because of the shriek-like chanting during prayer.

21. Which reformer is known as the "Frontier Gandhi", and which movement did he lead?

Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan — leader of the Khudai Khidmatgar Movement (1929) in the North-West Frontier Province. His volunteers wore red shirts.

22. Who founded the Rahnumai Mazdayasan Sabha, in which year, and for which community?

S. S. Bengali, Naoroji Furdonji, and J. B. Nacha, 1851 — for the Parsi community in Bombay, promoting Zoroastrian principles and modern education.

23. Who founded the Aligarh Movement, in which year, and what institution did it create?

Sir Syed Ahmad Khan — Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College, Aligarh, 1875; it became Aligarh Muslim University in 1920. His journal was Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq.

24. Name the two all-India women's organisations founded by Annie Besant and Sarala Devi Chaudhurani.

Annie Besant — Indian Women's Association (1917). Sarala Devi Chaudhurani — Bharat Stree Mandal (1910). Also: Pandita Ramabai's Arya Mahila Samaj (1882).
